## 1. What this video covers
A long, loosely structured walkthrough (filmed in Mexico) of his TradingView setup and technical approach, split into "beginner," "intermediate" and "advanced" advice. It covers how he marks support/resistance as **boxes around psychological/quarter levels** (GBPUSD 1.25 / 1.275 / 1.30 / 1.325 / 1.35), how to project take-profits by "cloning" a range, where stops belong (beyond the last lower high / higher low, not a fixed 10–20 pips), how intraday traders vs swing traders vs scalpers differ in timeframes and stop size, using an 8 / 14 moving average (or just the 8), black/monochrome charts, counter-trendline breaks, Fibonacci 61.8 and the -27 extension (AUDUSD daily), TradingView alerts and the TradingView news headlines. It ends with a 10-point "how to be the best trader you can be" list. It reflects the 2020 intraday/swing level-to-level version of his strategy. Roughly 18:17–21:19 and 39:33–47:37 are personal/motivational talk.

## 2. Trading style, holding period and timeframes
- Pick one style first; it decides your timeframe: "You can't be a scalper trying to just get 15, 20 pips and you're looking at a daily time frame; you also can't be a swing trader and you're looking at a one-minute time frame" [5:05]. Decide through backtesting and trial and error.
- Definitions he gives [26:23–27:24]:
  - **Swing trader**: lets the trade run to the target "over the next couple of days and [has] no worries"; uses the daily; wide stop with target "two, three, four times that" [28:25].
  - **Intraday (day) trader**: "looking to just get in, get their 30 to 100 pips, 30 to 90, 80 pips and get out for the day." Analyzes on the **daily or preferably the 4-hour**, confirms on the **1-hour or 2-hour**, and may hold ~10–11 hours within a 24-hour cycle (in the morning and out before day's end, or in at night and out within 24 hours). "Intraday traders use the five minute for entries but not for analyzing" [27:24].
  - **Scalper**: 1-minute / 5-minute, in and out every 10–30 minutes.
- He trades both swing and intraday (in this example: "if I'm swing trading, my take profit would 100% be right here; if I'm intraday trading I would obviously be scaling down into the two hour time frame or the one hour time frame" [22:20–23:21]).
- His moving averages are shown on the **2-hour** chart in the example [24:22].

## 3. Markets, pairs and sessions

- GBPUSD "moves more likely during London and New York, especially the overlap" [22:20].
- Keep all the pairs you trade in your TradingView watchlist [32:27]; "focus on what pair or pairs you're trading" [40:34].

## 4. Tools and indicators (with exact settings)
- **Platform**: TradingView. Bar Replay for backtesting (example speed 0.5 s) [6:05].
- **Moving averages**: "is it either the 8 and the 14 or the 8" [5:05]; "I use sometimes the eight and the fourteen, but sometimes ... the eight, where it's just black and just one moving average" [29:25]. Rule: below the MA = still selling, above = buying. Type (SMA/EMA) **not stated** here (refers to his earlier "trail moving average" [?] video). In the 2H example the colors were red and blue MAs [24:22].
- "I only use one indicator ... the moving average, and sometimes I take the moving averages off and just focus purely on price action" [39:33–40:34]. No RSI/MACD/stochastics/Bollinger.
- **Drawing tools he uses** [6:05–7:05]: trendline (heavily), horizontal line, horizontal ray (preferred — "a lot cleaner"), Fibonacci retracement ("from time to time"), rectangle/box, ellipse (to highlight higher lows etc.), text and callout notes (e.g. "price broke CTL" = counter-trendline) [38:32].
- **Box templates** saved by purpose: "breakout region," "liquidity area," red, "cool blue," black areas [11:08].
- **Fibonacci**: 61.8% retracement as the entry/bounce zone; **-27% extension** ("27 extension") as the first target and the -61.8% extension as the next [35:29].
- **Chart styles**: candlesticks (prefers them over Heikin Ashi) [9:05–10:08]; "black charts" (all-black candles) to remove the emotional effect of colors — "use black charts because it takes away the psychology" [29:25]; gold-colored up candles and black down candles when trading gold [8:05].
- **Alerts**: TradingView price alerts sent by SMS/email ("hey stupid, open trade me now") — "alerts are the biggest thing that you can use in trading ... it's definitely a cheat code" [32:27–34:29].
- **News**: TradingView headlines/news tab for the pair, for fundamental and sentiment context [36:30–37:32].
- **Quarter-levels theory**: he used it heavily before; still uses "a little bit of it to this day," "more modern and more to my style" [13:11], [21:19–22:20].

## 5. Analysis process, step by step
1. Set up TradingView with your pairs, chosen chart style and your one MA (8, or 8 and 14) [5:05], [32:27].
2. On the higher timeframe (daily/4H), mark where price "usually stopped": horizontal rays at wicks/turning points [12:09].
3. Mark psychological/quarter levels (e.g. 1.25, 1.275, 1.30, 1.325, 1.35 on GBPUSD) and **draw a box around each**, "because price will stop at an area and not a [exact price]" [13:11]. A level is confirmed when price bounces repeatedly, fails to close beyond the box, then breaks and retests it [14:12].
4. Mark the **midpoint** between two quarter levels (e.g. 1.325 between 1.30 and 1.35), and any level price just broke from that caused a sharp move [21:19–22:20].
5. Identify market structure: higher lows/lower highs, the last high/low, and the **break of structure** when price breaks the last swing; watch for the retest and a lower high (for shorts) [7:05–8:05].
6. Project targets: the next key level/box, or "clone" the height of the previous range below/above the breakout (the GBPUSD 1.35→1.30 range projected to 1.25) [14:12–15:14], [21:19].
7. Add confluence: MA position, Fibonacci 61.8 bounce and -27 extension, trendlines and counter-trendlines.
8. Check fundamentals and sentiment from the TradingView headlines [36:30].
9. Drop to the entry timeframe (1H/2H for intraday; 5-minute only for entry timing) and wait for the entry pattern; set alerts at levels instead of watching the chart [24:22], [32:27].

## 6. Entry rules
- Intraday "sniper" example (GBPUSD, 2H/1H): consolidation, then a **lower high** below the MA, then an **evening star** confirmation → enter, stop above the lower high [23:21–24:22].
- Long example: price bounces repeatedly at a support level; a **bullish engulfing above the moving average** and then a **retest of the moving average** → "you should be buying" [29:25].
- **Counter-trendline (CTL) break**: in a trend, on the pullback drop to the 2H and draw a counter-trendline along the retracement; "whenever price breaks this counter trendline, that is your entry" (an additional entry on top of the first) [30:25–31:26].
- **Re-entry on retracement**: set an alert at a retracement level (e.g. GBPUSD 1.28100 / 1.2750); when hit, look for "some kind of bullish [bearish] confirmation" and enter again [33:29–34:29].
- Don't chase (FOMO): entering after a 500-pip drop means your stop would technically need to go above the last high before the drop [16:15].
- Intraday traders and scalpers need precise ("sniper") entries because their stops are tighter; swing traders less so [27:24].
- Order type: not specified (examples read as market entries on confirmation; alerts are used to catch the moment).

## 7. Stop-loss rules
- Structural stop: "You need to have it above the last lower high in a downtrend and ... below the last higher low in the uptrend" [28:25–29:25].
- Add buffer: if the entry is close to the last lower high, "for good measure I'd place it above here because this is so close to here" [16:15].
- No fixed small stops: "Don't listen to people out there who say you need a 10 to 20 pip stop loss every time" [28:25].
- Give the trade "enough breathing room"; tight stops lead to being stopped out before price goes your way [17:16], [28:25].
- Swing traders use a large stop so that if hit, "they know it's the absolute wrong trade," with targets 2–4× the stop [28:25].

## 8. Take-profit and targets
- Take profit at the next support/resistance level/box: "it allows you to know where your take profit is always here" [30:25].
- Intraday: set the TP "at the exact level where price got to" previously (not beyond it) [24:22].
- Measured move: clone the prior range (top to bottom) and project it from the break (GBPUSD 1.35→1.30 → 1.25) [14:12–15:14].
- Midpoint (e.g. GBPUSD ~1.276 / 1.275) is a likely reaction point / alternative target if price doesn't reach the full level [22:20], [24:22–25:23].
- Counter-trendline break targets: "point C, point B and point A" — the points where the trendline started, because after a trendline break price "more times than not" returns to them [31:26].
- Fibonacci: -27 extension, then -61.8 extension [35:29].
- Intraday target size: 30–100 pips [26:23]. Swing: 2–4× the stop [28:25].

## 9. Trade management
- If price gets very close to the TP (e.g. ~10 pips) and stalls and you feel anxious, "feel free to just get out" rather than let it reverse; otherwise let it hit the TP [25:23].
- Swing traders ignore intraday noise; a lower-timeframe mess may just be "one big red candle" on the daily [31:26–32:27].
- Use alerts so you never need to watch the chart [32:27].
- Add a second entry on a counter-trendline break or on a retracement alert [31:26], [34:29].
- Break-even / partials: not covered.

## 10. Risk management and position sizing
- Risk % / lot size: not covered.
- R:R: swing targets are "two, three, four times" the stop [28:25].
- Intraday target 30–100 pips [26:23].
- Rules from his list: have a trading plan, no FOMO, no revenge trading, discipline/patience [39:33–40:34].

## 12. Rules, do's and don'ts
- Know your style first: "Figure out if you're a scalper, an intraday trader or a swing trader, and that only comes [from] back testing and trial and error" [5:05].
- Mark areas, not lines: "I like to draw a box around it just because price will stop at an area" [13:11].
- Stops go beyond structure: "You need to have it above the last lower high in a downtrend and below the last higher low in the uptrend" [28:25–29:25].
- No fixed tiny stops: "Don't listen to people out there who say you need a 10 to 20 pip stop loss every time" [28:25].
- Don't chase: entering late after a big move means a far stop; FOMO is the biggest killer [16:15].
- MA bias: "any time price is below here I'm still selling" (8 MA) [29:25].
- Use black charts: "it takes away the psychology behind it" [29:25].
- Use alerts: "alerts are the biggest thing that you can use in trading ... it's definitely a cheat code" [34:29].
- Combine technical and fundamental analysis (TradingView headlines) [39:33].
- Intraday: "use the five minute for entries but not for analyzing" [27:24].
- Near the target and stalling: "feel free to just get out" [25:23].
- Keep the chart simple; one indicator only [39:33–40:34].
- His 10-point list [38:32–44:36]: (1) figure out what type of trader you are; (2) figure out your timeframe; (3) have a trading plan; (4) combine technical and fundamental analysis; (5) use indicators or don't (he uses one); (6) focus on the pair(s) you trade; (7) consistency, discipline, patience, no FOMO — psychology; (8) don't give up; (9) double down on what works; (10) have a mentor.
