## 1. What this video covers
This is his explanation of why he switched to position trading in 2025. He tells a story about a trader who held a USDJPY position from 102 and would have made $3.5M had she not exited and reversed. He then describes reviewing his own trades (especially a large gold long) and backtesting longer holds. The method itself barely changes: the same Fibonacci and trend-line analysis, now done on the daily/weekly, with trades held for weeks to months, fewer than 10 trades a quarter, and partial profits along the way. He gives his recent win-rate figures and says wrong timing with 20-pip stops cost him about half his losses. Large parts are promotion for a $47 "5-day position trading intensive" (Aug 18) and a "position" document on Instagram, which are not documented here.

## 2. Trading style, holding period and timeframes
- **Style:** position trading ("positioning"). "Positioning, you don't have to worry about that. you're just focused on where is it going to get to whether it takes two weeks, whether it takes two months, where is it going to get to by the end of this quarter" [8:11].
- **Holding period:** weeks to months; he reviewed his trades asking what would happen with "an extra week, an extra month, an extra two months" [2:04].
- **Timeframes:** daily and weekly. "what does this want to do from a long-term point of view, from a macro point of view? Looking at a weekly or a daily" [3:07]. "doing it on a daily time frame and just looking at it and then getting in the trade and just holding it" [9:13].
- Against the lower timeframes: analysing on the daily/weekly "is a lot smoother because you see so much more than just a bunch of candles going up and down like you would on a fivem minute time frame" [4:07].
- You still monitor the trade, but "not looking for a day or two or three" [9:13].

## 3. Markets, pairs and sessions
- Gold is the main instrument he reviewed ("I went back and reviewed some of my trades, gold specifically" [2:04]).
- USDJPY in the opening story (102 to 120; it later reached 150) [0:03-1:04, 15:19].
- Sessions: not relevant to positioning; "you come in when you want. You take profit when you want" [18:22]. Day trading and scalping force you to "change your schedule to fit the market schedules" [18:22].

## 4. Tools and indicators (with exact settings)
- Same tools as before: "using Fibonacci trend lines all the same stuff but doing it on a daily time frame" [9:13].
- TradingView mentioned for reviewing charts [15:19].
- No indicator settings given in this video.

## 5. Analysis process, step by step
1. Look at the market from a macro/long-term view on the weekly or daily [3:07].
2. Apply the same Fibonacci and trend-line analysis on the daily [9:13].
3. Decide where price is likely to get to "by the end of this quarter", not when [8:11].
4. Pick only the few best setups ("two good pitches, three good pitches, five good pitches, 10 good pitches over a quarter" [8:11]).
- No more detail on the process in this video (he refers to his intensive and the "position" document).

## 6. Entry rules
- Not covered in this video in terms of exact triggers or order types. The only entry principle: wait for the best pitch: "I wait for the pitch to come to me. I'm not swinging at every single pitch" (paraphrasing Warren Buffett) [5:07].

## 7. Stop-loss rules
- Stated problem with his old stops: of his last 130 trades, in "at least 81" his direction was right but "like half of my loss because why? I had a 20 pip stop loss. I got in too late. I got in too early" [7:09-8:11]. With positioning "you don't have to worry about that" [8:11].
- No new stop size is given.

## 8. Take-profit and targets
- The target is where price gets to over the quarter [8:11]. He uses big moves as examples: USDJPY 102 to 120 (and 150) [0:03-1:04, 15:19]; gold "skyrocketed 10,000 pips" after his trade [3:07].
- No Fibonacci target levels named in this video.

## 9. Trade management
- **Hold the trade:** the moral of the USDJPY story is to not touch the position: "She just let it ride" [1:04].
- **Partial profits while holding:** "You can still take partial profits. If you're up 10, 20, 30, $40,000, you can literally go to your phone or computer and literally take partial profits. Take 10% out, 20, 30, 40% out and still let the trade run" [9:13-10:16].
- **Weekend holding and costs:** "We're talking about commissions, rollover fees, swap. Like, pay it. Hold it over the weekend. Pay it" [15:19].
- Monitor, but don't watch every candle [9:13].

## 10. Risk management and position sizing
- **Trade frequency:** "literally take less than 10 trades a quarter" [5:07]. "Can you imagine how much simpler life would be if you took 10 trades in three months as opposed to 10 trades in one day?" [8:11].
- **Win-rate claims since switching:** "I won 10 out of my last 12 trades ... I won 20 out of my last 24 trades ... I won my last 30 out of my last 38 trades" [9:13].
- **Old record:** "at least 81 trades out of my last 130 where I literally said the market was going to do this and it did that" [7:09].
- **Lot size example:** a 50-lot gold position (see Trade 2).
- Goal framing: "I would rather make a million dollars a year off of six trades ... versus making a million dollars a year off of 400 trades" [18:22].
- No risk % stated.

## 11. Trades walked through in this video

### Trade 1: USDJPY long (someone else's position trade) (0:03-1:04, 15:19)
- **Context:** a woman trader, late 2020 / early 2021, believed USDJPY would go from 102.00 to 120.00. She was a position trader.
- **What happened:** price went 102 to 108; she exited, "secured that 700 racks" ($700,000), then reversed and shorted. Price kept going to 120. She lost the $700k and more. She said she "would have made $3.5 million had she just stayed in the trade" [1:04]. USDJPY later reached 150, so holding further "would have been 8 n 10 million" [15:19].
- **Lesson:** one trade on one pair, held, beats many trades.

### Trade 2: Gold (XAUUSD) long, his own (2:04-3:07)
- **Position:** "I had a 50 lot. A 50 lot on gold" [3:07].
- **Status at the time:** "I was up over $40,000. And my goal was to get it to $108,000" [2:04]. He implies he exited (it is a "what if I had held" review).
- **What-if:** gold "has skyrocketed 10,000 pips" since; held until now it would be "$5 million. Not 500K, 5 million" [3:07].
- **Entry, stop, exit price:** not stated.
- **Note:** the "$40,000 up, goal $108,000" details match the live gold trade in video 7YmARnoIJ7I (uploaded 2025-03-02), where his maximum profit was $108,000 and the trade peaked at about $40,000. But that video shows about 22.5 lots, not 50, so it may or may not be the same trade [?].

## 12. Rules, do's and don'ts
- Trade less: "People won't tell you, but you're actually a good trader. You're just swinging too many times" [4:07].
- Wait for the pitch: "every single pitch is not worth swinging" [5:07].
- Fewer than 10 trades a quarter: "literally take less than 10 trades a quarter" [5:07].
- Hold for the full move: "where is it going to get to by the end of this quarter" [8:11].
- Take partials but keep the runner: "Take 10% out, 20, 30, 40% out and still let the trade run" [10:16].
- Pay the swap and hold over weekends: "Hold it over the weekend. Pay it" [15:19].
- If you call direction right but get stopped: "you know how to trade. You just don't know how to day trade" [7:09].

## 13. Changes from his earlier approach (as he states them)
- **Headline:** "I have been trading wrong for the last few years" [0:03]. The story "is why I kind of switched my entire strategy around" [3:07].
- **From day/swing to position:** in Secure the Swag season 2 (LA) he was "still ... trading more like a day trader and then like slowly getting into swing trading" [1:04]. Now he holds weeks to months.
- **Why:**
  1. Reviewing past trades showed that holding an extra week/month/two months would have made far more (gold: $5M vs ~$40k) [2:04-3:07].
  2. Too-tight 20-pip stops and bad timing caused about half his losses even when direction was right (81 of 130 trades) [7:09-8:11].
  3. Backtesting and live testing gave 10/12, 20/24, 30/38 wins [9:13].
  4. Lifestyle: positioning fits around life; day trading forces you onto market hours [14:19, 18:22].
  5. Influences: Warren Buffett ("Day trading is a no strike game" paraphrase [4:07]; a Buffett book saying day trading is an uphill battle [12:18]), and Alex Hormozi's (captioned "Alexi") advice to double down on strengths [7:09].
- **What stayed the same:** "using Fibonacci trend lines all the same stuff but doing it on a daily time frame" [9:13].
- **When:** "this is something I developed earlier this year. I've kind of kept it more to myself. I didn't feel it was fair to make a video on it if I didn't back test it myself" [17:21]. He also says he wishes he had started "in 2021 and 2022" [10:16].
- Learning time: "all you got to do is learn for a ... couple of weeks, let's say eight weeks of of an intensive study" [18:22].
