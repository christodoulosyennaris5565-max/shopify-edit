## 1. What this video covers
A short (13-minute) argument for position trading over day trading and scalping, using the same weekly-vs-15-minute "bar replay" exercise he uses with mentorship students. He shows that the same Fibonacci A-B-C structure is easy to read on the weekly chart and unreadable on the 15-minute chart. He gives the success-rate figures he quotes for each trading style, a real GBPAUD short he called in his mentorship in mid-March 2026 (about 900 pips over about two months), and lot-size and pip profit maths. He states his current trade frequency (5-6 trades a month) and holding period (anywhere from 5 to 14 days up to a couple of months). He ends with the "check your old trades 3 weeks later" P&L exercise. This is his current (2026) position-trading strategy. There is very little on entry or stop mechanics.

## 2. Trading style, holding period and timeframes
- **Style:** position trading, always with the higher-timeframe (weekly) trend. Day trading on the 15-minute chart is the approach he says he "used to" use and that cost him years.
- **Analysis timeframe:** weekly. "If I just went to the weekly time frame … it's a lot easier to figure that out" [6:07]. The 15-minute (and 5-minute) chart is the counter-example: unpredictable noise.
- **Holding period:** flexible. "I'm going to take five trades a month, six trades a month, and … hold them" [10:15]. "You can alter it for 5 days, 7 days, 14 days, 12 days" [10:15]. He suggests beginners "start small" with holds of about 8-12 days rather than two months. Those who are more patient can hold for months; the GBPAUD example was held for about two months.
- He explains why the 15-minute chart is misleading: four 15-minute candles make an hour, and you need 24 hours of them for a day and a full week of them for a week [2:05]. A big 15-minute drop can just be the "C" leg of a weekly ABC.

## 5. Analysis process, step by step
1. Start on the **weekly** timeframe and identify the overall trend and structure (e.g. a Fibonacci A-B-C pattern pointing to a D leg) [1:04].
2. Decide which way the market is going on that higher timeframe. Trade only in that direction ("trading with the overall trend") [1:04, 6:07].
3. Ignore 15-minute and 5-minute moves against that direction. They are part of the larger structure, not a new trend [2:05].
4. Use psychological levels as the waypoints/targets (GA: past one psych level, down to the next) [8:14].
5. Filter by how clean the chart looks: skip ugly charts (gold, July 2026) and trade the clean trend (GA) [8:14].
6. Enter, hold for days to weeks, and let the trade run (5-6 trades a month) [10:15].

## 6. Entry rules
- Enter in the direction of the weekly trend only. A counter-trend 15-minute signal is a trap.
- In the GA example he told mentorship students "GA is coming back up. We need to enter. We should enter right now basically" [6:07]. This reads as selling into the pullback up within a weekly downtrend. The exact entry price, order type (market vs limit) and confirmation are not stated.
- Not covered in this video: limit orders, Fib entry levels, confirmation candles.

## 11. Trades walked through in this video

### Trade 1: Weekly vs 15-minute Fibonacci ABC (illustration) (1:04–2:05)
- A drawn sketch, not a real trade. On the weekly, price makes A, B, C and then goes up to D: an obvious buy.
- The same pattern on a 15-minute chart, viewed at point C, looks like "the biggest drop ever and a little bit going up", so a 15-minute trader reads it as a downtrend and sells. The point: timeframe context decides the read.

### Trade 2: Bar-replay exercise, unnamed pair, 15-minute (5:07–6:07)
