## 1. What this video covers
He presents position trading as his "primary strategy … for 2026 into 2027" and argues that other strategies, "including my former one" (day trading / screen-glued trading), are trash. Most of the video is conceptual. It covers the "check where price was 3 weeks after you exited" P&L exercise, matching analysis timeframe to trading style, and a bar-replay game on USDCAD (weekly 3-month prediction vs 15-minute 5-hour prediction). It also covers his core rule of only trading in the direction you expect price to be in 3 months (invalidated only by a break in market structure), wide stops vs 20-30 pip drawdown, lot sizing, and why you should use six-figure prop accounts. He sketches his weekly routine: analyse on Sunday/Monday, place a buy/sell limit, 1-2 trades in that direction, move the stop to break-even then into profit, check periodically. He says a full step-by-step technical breakdown will come in a separate video (that is the 2026-04-04 masterclass, 787JlWMtCwU).

## 2. Trading style, holding period and timeframes
- **Style:** position trading on the macro/higher timeframes. "Trading is simply looking at the macro side of things and predicting where it goes. And most of you guys look at the micro, but want the macro side of money" [16:20].
- **Directional horizon:** 3 months. "If you believe it's going to be there in 3 months, why is every single trade you take not in that direction?" [13:19].
- **Holding:** not literally 3 months. "I'm not telling you to look at what the markets are going to do over the next 3 months and just hold it for 3 months" [13:19]. You can take "10 trades over those 3 months" [14:19]. The benchmark exercise compares results with holding for about 3-4 weeks.
- **Timeframe must match style:** don't analyse on 5-minute and then swing trade, analyse on 1-minute while holding for days, or start a day-trade analysis on the weekly [8:13].
- The weekly chart is used for the 3-month read [11:18].

## 5. Analysis process, step by step
1. On the weekly/higher timeframe, decide the direction you expect over the next **3 months** [10:16–13:19].
2. Mark the key zone (box) and trend line that price may test or break (USDCAD example) [32:32].
3. Take every trade in that direction only. Buy only if bullish, sell only if bearish, no counter-trend [13:19–14:19].
4. Change the bias only if **market structure breaks**: "the market comes down and then up and then down again, lower high, lower low, boom, lower high, lower low. Then it's like, 'Oh, we're clearly in a downtrend'" [32:32].
5. Do the analysis once, on a Sunday or Monday: "you can just look at a chart on one Monday or Sunday, analyze for a good amount of time, and … place a buy limit or sell limit" [33:32–34:32].
6. Place one or two trades in that direction and let price come to you [34:32].
7. Check periodically over the next couple of days and manage (break-even → profit) [34:32].

## 6. Entry rules
- Only in the 3-month direction [13:19–14:19].
- **Order type:** buy limit or sell limit placed after the weekend analysis. "Place it, let it, you know, kind of come to you, place, you know, one or two trades in that direction" [34:32].
- No sniper entries: "as a position trader, you're not really worried about your entries" [17:22]. Entries are said not to be the problem; "your dedication … to trying to push through a failing concept is" [17:22].
- Detailed entry levels (Fib) not covered here.

## 7. Stop-loss rules
- Wide stops with breathing room. Example: "This is a 80-pip stop loss" against "well over … 300 pips" TP [17:22–18:22].
- "I'm not worried about a 20-pip 30-pip drawdown, but some of you guys have a 20-pip 30-pip stop loss, so being 5 pips in … drawdown scares you out" [18:22].
- The bar-replay game shows 15-minute traders who "they're not going to hold 50 pips in drawdown. They're going to either get out or get stopped out" [12:19].

## 8. Take-profit and targets
- Target the 3-month objective level (USDCAD: 1.4050 / 1.4150, "up here around 141-ish" by about June) [32:32].
- Example R:R: 80-pip SL vs about 313-pip TP = "1 and 3.92, basically 1 and 4" [18:22].
- No TP ladder in this video.

## 9. Trade management
- "Periodically check it over the next couple of days, move your stop loss into break even, then move your stop loss into profit, so it's a risk-free trade, and just let it get there" [34:32].
- Hold through early drawdown. In the December GBPUSD call, students were 30-40 pips down for 2 days before a 200-250 pip move [18:22].
- Hold winners longer. The 3-weeks-later exercise is about how much more holding would have made. The goal is to capture "20% … 30% … 50%" more of it, not all of it [5:11–6:12].

## 10. Risk management and position sizing
- **"Your lot sizes are wrong"** is named as a real reason for not being profitable [22:25]. Exercise: imagine every past trade at double the lot (2 → 4, 1 → 2, 0.5 → 1) [22:25]. His point is that sizing correctly on a large account multiplies results (his student example below).
- **Pip value:** 2 lots = $20/pip [4:10].
- **Prop-firm account size:** avoid small accounts with tiny drawdown limits. "trade a 10K prop firm account when the drawdown the max drawdown is like what? $400? Right? 4%? 5%?" [30:28]. Use 100K (drawdown $4,000-$5,000) or 500K: "your daily drawdown is 25K … Your max drawdown is 50K" [31:31]. "It is much better to take the next month and a half to stack up money and then get a 500K account" than keep blowing 5K/10K accounts with a $250 daily loss limit [31:31].
- Persistence: the student who made $471K had blown five prior accounts (10K, 25K, 50K: all under six figures) [30:28].
- **Polls he cites:** 96% would rather earn $500K/yr with free time than $750K glued to screens [7:12]. Barriers: 32% stress, 45% entries/exits, 5% lot size, 80% "I don't know what I'm doing sometimes" [16:20].
- No explicit risk-% per trade in this video.
