## 1. What this video covers
Sniper entries part 1, done in TradingView bar-replay. He explains why he does not wait for a full break of a consolidation box: when price consolidates at resistance (or support), he expects the opposite direction and, on the 15-minute, enters on the first break of structure inside the box confirmed by an MA crossover. Stops go above the last lower high (e.g. ~10 pips), targets go to the next key level (1:13, 1:7, 1:3, 1:7 examples). He then trails the stop to each new lower high to make the trade risk-free, and scales in on later lower highs/MA crossovers. Examples on EURUSD (resistance 1.1250) and GBPAUD (round-number levels 1.85/1.80/1.75). This is the 2020 day-trading version.

## 5. Analysis process, step by step
1. Mark the key level (e.g. 1.1250 resistance on EURUSD; round numbers on GBPAUD) and set an alert [1:05, 12:18].
2. On the 2-hour, see price consolidating at the level; draw a box around it [1:05].
3. Infer direction: consolidation at resistance → expect down; at support → expect up [2:07].
4. Wait for a break of structure (a lower high and lower low) within the box — not a full break of the box [2:07–3:10].
5. Scale to 15-minute; draw a smaller box around the local consolidation; watch for fake-outs of the box [3:10–4:10].
6. Wait for MA crossover plus break of structure; he waits for "one more" confirming candle [4:10].
7. Enter; stop above last lower high; target next level from the 2-hour [4:10].
8. Watch for "little candlesticks" forming at the top/bottom of a range as a sign of an imminent move [13:19].

## 6. Entry rules
- Don't wait for the box to break: "a lot of traders will say wait for it to break the box and then get in — that's not a sniper entry" [1:05–2:07].
- Need: consolidation at the key level + break of structure (lower high/lower low for shorts; higher low for longs) + MA crossover [3:10–4:10, 9:12].
- Fake breaks of the small box are ignored; "I'm waiting for a moving average crossover but also a break of structure" [3:10].
- Also valid: bearish engulfing followed by an MA crossover [13:19].
- Re-entry/scale-in: after the trade moves and price makes a new lower high and the MA crosses back down, take a second trade [8:11, 16:23].
- Re-entry after a stop-out in profit: wait for the retest of the box, 15-minute consolidation, MA crossover + break of structure [10:14].
- A lower high that fails to exceed the prior lower high = short entry [14:20–15:22].
- Be aggressive: "you cannot be afraid to get in" [17:24].

## 7. Stop-loss rules
- "Stop-loss is above the last lower high" [4:10].
- Example size: "you'd have like a 10 pip stop loss and a 90 pip profit" [11:18].
- "A few pips above" the lower high, or above the box [9:12, 15:22].
- Stops must sit at structure, not at random points: "you can't just move it to a random point" [7:10].

## 8. Take-profit and targets
- Next key level from the 2-hour [4:10].
- Can use two targets: first TP at a nearer level (1:3), second TP at the next round-number level (e.g. 1.75) [13:19–14:20].
- Minimum R:R 1:3: "I don't trade 1 to 1… or 1 to 2" [5:10]; "anything less than 1:3 I'm not trading" [14:20].
- May close manually close to the level [17:24].

## 9. Trade management
- Trail the stop: once in profit and price retraces then continues, move the stop to the high of that retracement (new lower high); repeat at each new lower high [5:10–7:10, 17:24].
- Rationale: price "can't break structure" — it won't go above that lower high and still continue down [6:10].
- Move it from "-$200 or -$1,000 depending on your account" to locked profit [6:10].
- Once stop is above entry the trade is "risk free" [8:11].
- Scale in on later lower highs + MA crossovers; multiple positions share the trailing stop [8:11, 16:23].
- After a TP, wait and "play it by ear" whether price breaks the level or reverses [14:20].

## 10. Risk management and position sizing
- Lot size example: "with a two or three or four lot size" a locked-in worst case is "probably a thousand, two thousand dollars"; best case "five grand, six grand" [6:10–7:10].
- Win rate: "if you trade a 1:3, 1:4, 1:5 risk reward ratio you can lose 70% of your trades and still make thousands every single week" [5:10].
- No risk % stated.
- Says he has been trading "five years" [2:07].

## 11. Trades walked through in this video
### Trade 1: EURUSD short (1:05–9:12)
- Context: 2-hour; resistance 1.12500 with alert; price consolidating in a box at resistance → bearish.
- Entry: 15-minute; smaller box; fake-out below ignored; waited for break of structure + MA crossover + "one more" candle → short [price shown on chart].
- Stop: above the last lower high [shown].
- Target: next level on 2-hour [shown]; 1:13 R:R.
- Management: price dropped; on the first pullback and continuation, stop moved to the pullback high (worst case ~$1–2k profit with 2–4 lots); then trailed at each new lower high. Second entry possible on the next lower high + MA cross back down.
- Result: eventually "price stopped me out" in profit — "I made a couple grand."

### Trade 3: GBPAUD short (11:18–14:20)

- Result: target reached around 1.75 (he says he'd probably have closed manually near it).

## 12. Rules, do's and don'ts
- Don't wait for the box break: "that's not a sniper entry" [1:05].
- Consolidation at a level = opposite move: "if it's purely consolidating at resistance or at support, chances are it's going the opposite way" [2:07].
- Trigger: "I'm waiting for a moving average crossover but also a break of structure" [3:10].
- "Stop-loss is above the last lower high" [4:10].
- "I don't trade 1 to 1 risk to reward ratio or 1 to 2" [5:10].
- Trail at structure: "you keep moving your stop-loss every time it retraces" [6:10].
- Not random stops: "you can't just move it to a random point" [7:10].
- "Anything less than 1 to 3 I'm not trading" [14:20].
- "You have to be aggressive when you see price break structure… you cannot be afraid to get in" [17:24].
- No perfect entries: "there's no such thing as a perfect entry" — get "as close to perfect as possible" [2:07].
