## 1. What this video covers
He explains psychological (round-number) price levels, which he calls "the main way I trade," using GBPUSD on the 4H chart from late October to mid-December 2020. He draws every whole-figure level (1.29, 1.30 ... 1.35) and shows price moving from one round number to the next, consolidating at them and reversing at them. The video is mainly about using those levels as **take-profit targets** ("trading from level to level"), with one worked entry example (higher low + moving average break on the 4H). It reflects his 2020 intraday/swing style: 1-3 pairs, ~100-pip moves held hours to a couple of days, a single moving average and otherwise pure price action. The section on "quarter levels" was moved to his paid academy and is not in this video.

## 2. Trading style, holding period and timeframes
- He calls himself both an intraday trader and a swing trader, not a scalper: "I'm not a scalper, I'm an intraday trader and I'm a swing trader, I'm both" [20:20]. He can close the same day or the next day.
- Typical holding period for one level-to-level move: "it may take a day or two" — he lists "15 hours, 10 hours, 32 hours" as examples [20:20]. He does not hold for weeks: "would I just hold it over the course of a few weeks? No, get in, get out by the next day" [21:21].
- Holding over the 5 pm (New York) "market reset" / rollover is fine; swap is only "10 cents, a dollar, 50 cents, two dollars, three dollars" [20:20–21:21].
- Timeframes: the **4-hour** for the overall view and levels; **entries on the 1-hour and 15-minute** [19:19]. Reason: on the 4H "you may miss a move" because a 4H candle takes long to form.
- For the daily/weekly view, the broader "quarter levels" (e.g. 1.30, 1.325, 1.35) matter more; for intraday, every whole figure (1.29, 1.30, 1.31 ...) matters [4:10].

## 3. Markets, pairs and sessions
- Example instrument: GBPUSD (4H; he also flips to the daily for one comparison).
- Pair count: "you really can just trade one, two or three pairs at the most and not have to juggle six to ten pairs because you kind of know the levels" [19:19–20:20].
- Sessions: no specific session rule. He mentions not entering just before major news (hypothetical 2 pm release, entry at 1:45 pm) and notes that 1:45 pm is "closer to the market reset, so nobody usually gets in that late" [18:19] (implying he does not usually enter late in the New York afternoon).

## 4. Tools and indicators (with exact settings)
- **One moving average** — "the only indicator I have is a moving average" [21:21]. Period/type **not stated** in this video [shown on chart, not stated]. Sometimes he removes even that and reads "pure price action."
- Horizontal lines/rays at round numbers. On his live chart he keeps only the 2-3 levels nearest price and deletes the far ones; the fully lined chart in the video was made for illustration [2:08], [16:18].
- Trendlines (drawn off higher lows / lower highs).
- Chart patterns used as context: inverted head and shoulders, consolidation, doji, evening star [?] (probably meant morning star; see Gaps).
- News: TradingView's "News" tab (third tab) for GBPUSD/Brexit updates and Kitco. He does **not** use Forex Factory [12:13–13:13].
- Uses TradingView's drawing/box tools to measure.

## 5. Analysis process, step by step
1. On the 4H chart, mark the whole-number psychological levels around current price (e.g. 1.31, 1.32, 1.33 on GBPUSD). Keep only the levels near price; delete those far away [16:18].
2. Look back in history to see how price reacted at each level (e.g. GBPUSD 1.35 rejected repeatedly; it had not been broken since May 2018) [14:14].
3. Read market structure at the level: consolidation, higher lows / lower highs, patterns like an inverted head and shoulders at a level ("we are only looking for buys right now") [5:11–7:12].
4. Draw a trendline off the higher lows/lower highs; note when price respects it or breaks it.
5. Check the moving average: break above/below it and retests of it are used as confirmation.
6. Check fundamentals/news (TradingView news tab, Kitco) for scheduled events and the big story (Brexit). A level that has held for years needs "something monumental" to break [14:14–15:16].
7. Set the target at the next round number (~100 pips on GBPUSD) and look for an entry on the 1H/15m [16:18], [19:19].
8. Stay flexible: if price stalls before the expected level (doji, consolidation, lower high), take profit and consider trading the other direction [8:13–9:13].

## 6. Entry rules
- This video is "a take profit level [video]; it's not so much a sniper entry level" [9:13–10:13]; entries are covered in his "sniper entry" videos (parts 1–4).
- Example entry pattern given [10:13]: a higher low at the level, a small doji/star reversal candle, then a "blue" (bullish) candle that pushes **above the moving average** → market entry (example: 1.32103 on GBPUSD).
- Break-and-retest of a round number is a recurring pattern, but "sometimes price won't re-test the level" [9:13].
- Entries are taken on the 1H or 15m chart after the 4H sets the scene (e.g. consolidation at a major psychological level, then a break above the MA) [19:19].
- Avoid entering just before major news [18:19].
- Order type: not specified (example reads as a market entry on candle confirmation).

## 7. Stop-loss rules
- Example: "stop loss a little bit below, like 30 pips below, of the last low" [10:13]. No other stop rule stated.

## 8. Take-profit and targets
- "My take profit for me is always the next level, because it's 100 pips" [16:18] — the next whole number on GBPUSD.
- Price often stops a few pips short of or beyond the exact round number (e.g. 1.33977 for 1.34; 1.3213, 1.3210, 1.31998) — treat these as the same level [11:13].
- Example R:R: he first said 1:3, then corrected himself to **1:2** (≈30-pip stop, ~100-pip move to 1.33 — he says "a little bit below"; the numbers he gives don't fully match 1:2, see Gaps) [10:13].

## 9. Trade management
- Trade one level at a time; don't hold the whole multi-week move: "You wouldn't hold this trade all the way to here, no. We get it from one level to the next" [20:20].
- Be willing to switch sides: close buys at a level when the market turns and then sell — "it's okay to be on both sides of the market" (he means sequentially, not hedging, which he says is illegal in the US) [8:13].
- Hold through the daily 5 pm reset if needed; swap cost is small [20:20–21:21].
- No break-even/partials rules mentioned.

## 10. Risk management and position sizing
- Risk % / lot sizes: not covered.
- R:R example 1:2 [10:13].
- Prefers ~100-pip targets over chasing 20–30 pips: "people are trying to get 20, 30 pips ... a hundred is an intraday slash swing style of trading" [16:18].
- Trade only 1–3 pairs [19:19].

## 11. Trades walked through in this video

### Trade 1: GBPUSD down-move into 1.29 and reversal (late Oct – early Nov 2020) (5:11–7:12)
- 4H chart. Price drops, retests 1.31, drops to 1.30, retests it along with a trendline, breaks below and falls to 1.29.
- At 1.29 it consolidates about five days (around Oct 27/28 to Nov 2/3) and forms an inverted head and shoulders — "a major key area"; at 1.30 there was only quick consolidation and lower highs.
- He says the US election (Nov 3) spikes should not be studied too much, but price still respected 1.30 and 1.31 and then made a higher low, from which a rising trendline can be drawn. Conclusion: "we are only looking for buys right now."
- No specific entry/stop given — context example.

### Trade 2: GBPUSD level-to-level climb 1.31 → 1.35 (Nov – Dec 2020) (7:12–12:13)
- After the election: breaks 1.31, retests, consolidates, breaks to 1.32, pulls back, breaks to 1.33, pulls back to 1.32 (lower high, retest of the moving average), drops to 1.31, then back up to 1.32, retest, up to 1.33; then above 1.33 to 1.34 "on the dot" (1.33977); a news wick, ~a week to 10 days of consolidation, then ranging 1.33–1.34 and on to 1.35, retested twice.
- His explanation: traders take profit at each round number, so price stalls/pulls back there.
- Each leg is a separate trade lasting from ~10 hours to ~2 days [20:20].

### Trade 3: GBPUSD long from 1.32 to 1.33 (10:13–11:13)
- 4H. Setup: higher low at 1.32, a small doji / reversal candle, then a bullish candle pushing above the moving average.
- Entry: 1.32103 (market, on the confirmation candle).
- Stop: about 30 pips below the last low.
- Target: 1.33 (next round number).
- Result: hit target with "literally zero drawdown except for ... a little bit of a wick." He calls it 1:2 R:R (after first saying 1:3).

### Trade 4: GBPUSD short from 1.35 (Brexit call) (13:13–15:16)
- Context: price reached 1.35, a level not broken since May 2018 and that "every time price got to 1.35 it just collapsed." He checked this on the daily.
- He predicted in an earlier "Secure the Swag" video: if Brexit talks fail → drop to 1.32; if they succeed → 1.36–1.37. Talks stalled; GBPUSD "dropped dramatically."
- Entry/stop not stated; target 1.32 as predicted.

### Trade 5: GBPUSD current outlook (12:13, 16:18)
- Last day of the trading week; price near 1.32/1.31. He expects it may rise a bit next week "maybe even 1.33" and then possibly collapse if Brexit news gets worse; keeps 1.31 on the chart as the next downside level. No entry given.

## 12. Rules, do's and don'ts
- Use whole-number levels as targets for intraday: "these simple round numbers ... 129, 130, 131 ... are very very very important and they can help you figure out your take profit" [4:10].
- Target the next level: "my take profit for me is always the next level because it's 100 pips" [16:18].
- Keep your chart clean: "I always ... delete them when [price is] not close" [16:18].
- Trade few pairs: "you really can just trade one, two or three pairs at the most" [19:19].
- Enter on lower timeframes: "I enter on the one hour and a 15 minute chart because on the four hour you may miss a move" [19:19].
- Don't hold for weeks: "get in, get out by the next day" [21:21].
- Be on the right side, not right in your prediction: "it's okay to be on both sides of the market" [8:13].
- Strong historical levels need major news to break: "regular retail sales and policies ... is not strong enough to break a level that hasn't been broken in three years" [14:14–15:16].
- Don't enter right before major news [18:19].
- Minimal indicators: "the only indicator I have is a moving average" [21:21].
- Don't use Forex Factory; use TradingView's news tab and Kitco [13:13].
