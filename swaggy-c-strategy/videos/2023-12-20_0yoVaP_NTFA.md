## 1. What this video covers
He walks through his trading routine. He starts his day around the Asian session (about 7:00 pm US Eastern), reviews a core watch list of four pairs, and enters swing trades between 7:30 and 9:30 pm ET. He enters early, at market, so he does not miss the move, and he accepts drawdown. Then he walks away and relies on stop loss, take profit and TradingView alerts. If price reaches a better level near his stop he adds a smaller second position. Once price makes a new lower high (on a sell) he moves the stop there, so the trade is risk-free, and adds a third, larger position. He takes profit around the New York session or trails the stop and holds for 2-7 days. This is his 2023 swing / Asian-session version. He contrasts it with his 2020 "sniper entry" style.

## 2. Trading style, holding period and timeframes
- He calls himself a swing trader who "can afford to be in drawdown" [2:06].
- Holding period: "sometimes these moves may take two, three, four, five, six, seven days" [9:11]. His most recent trade was entered on a Monday and stayed in drawdown until Wednesday, then went into big profit.
- He does not chase "sniper entries" anymore: "I don't care about sniper entries, right, I did in 2020" [8:11]. He goes for "the most pips" and tries not to miss trades.
- No chart timeframes are named in this video.

## 3. Markets, pairs and sessions
- Watch list: "I only like have four on my watch list each week that I'm taking seriously" [2:06]. There are usually 8 or 9 pairs on the list in total. The other 4 or 5 serve as confluences for the four main pairs. If one main pair "looks ugly" he swaps in another one.
- Pairs named: gold (XAUUSD), GBPAUD, GBPUSD, AUDUSD, and sometimes EURJPY [1:04-2:06].
- Session: his day starts around the Asian session. He comes in around 7:00 pm, sometimes doing a webinar in his paid group ("the lab").
- Entry window: "I usually enter my trades from between 7:30 and 9:30 at night, sometimes 10:30 ... I'm talking Eastern Standard Time" [7:10].
- Why Asian: liquidity is lowest, but "I find better setups that way", and with a calm market he can enter and relax [1:04]. The typical pattern he describes: a small move against him during Asia, then "London just massively reversed" in his direction [8:11].
- Exits: "I usually either take profit at the New York session or continue to move ... my stop loss in the profit" [9:11].

## 4. Tools and indicators (with exact settings)
- TradingView price alerts: "TradingView alerts works miracles" [6:08]. He sets them near his stop-loss level (e.g. 1950/1952 on a gold short with the stop at 1961) to catch a possible reversal there.
- Psychological ("psych") levels, e.g. gold 1950 [6:08].
- Market structure (lower highs) for stop placement and adding.
- No indicator settings are given in this video.

## 5. Analysis process, step by step
1. Around 7:00 pm ET, open the charts (sometimes live in a lab webinar) and go through the watch list pairs [1:04].
2. Focus on the four core pairs and use the other 4-5 pairs on the list as confluence. Swap out a pair that looks ugly [2:06].
3. Look for a setup where the confluences "already playing out" point one way, even if price is "not ready yet" [2:06].
4. Decide entry, stop and target in advance: "if I lose it's going to be here, if I win it's going to be here" [5:08]. Write it into the trading plan.
5. Enter during the Asian session (7:30-9:30 pm ET, sometimes 10:30), set alerts, then walk away [7:10].

## 6. Entry rules
- **Entry 1 – market execution, early.** If the confluences are there and he believes the direction, he enters right away rather than waiting: "I will literally enter for a sell right then right there because I don't want to miss the trade" [2:06-3:06]. He expects drawdown after this entry.
- **Entry 2 – smaller add at the better level.** If an alert near his stop fires and he sees price "print a reversal" at a psychological level (e.g. a bounce off 1950 that tried to wick up and came back down), he enters again: "that second position is a little bit lighter even though it's the better entry" [6:08], "whether it's half the lot size" [7:10]. Reason: he does not want to double his exposure while already in drawdown.
- **Entry 3 – bigger add after structure.** Once price comes back past entry 1, "creates more structure and gives me a lower high", he moves the stop to that lower high (positions 1 and 2 are now risk-free). Then he enters "with a way bigger lot size" because "this is the best possible entry" [6:08-7:10].
- He tells lab members who don't like drawdown to "be a little bit more patient and then get in when the market feels right to you" [4:07].

## 7. Stop-loss rules
- Set a fixed stop before entry and accept drawdown up to it. The market "can only do one of two things: hit that take profit or hit that stop loss" [5:08].
- Example stop: gold short at 1943, stop 1961 (about 18 points [~180 pips in his gold pip convention]).
- After a new lower high forms (shorts), move the stop to that lower high to make the trade risk-free [6:08].
- Keep moving the stop into profit while holding [9:11].

## 8. Take-profit and targets
- Example target: gold short from 1943 to 1910 or 1900 flat, which he estimated "should be a good one-to-four reward ratio ... just off the top of my head" [2:06]. (Arithmetic check: 33-43 points of reward against 18 points of risk is about 1:1.8-1:2.4, not 1:4. He was estimating loosely.)
- Takes profit at the New York session, or trails the stop and holds for days [9:11].

## 9. Trade management
- Set and forget: after entering he is "off my phone" and does not watch the trade. He only finds out in the morning [7:10-8:11].
- He uses a TradingView alert near the stop to know when to consider entry 2 [6:08].
- Scaling: smaller (≈half) second entry in drawdown, then a much bigger third entry once there is a new lower high and the stop has moved there [6:08-7:10].
- Moves the stop into profit and holds for 2-7 days, or closes at New York [9:11].
- Doesn't trade on vacation [10:13].

## 10. Risk management and position sizing
- Lot size must be small enough that a loss doesn't hurt. Traders get emotional in drawdown "because you place way too high of a lot size that if you lose you're going to blow your account or blow half your account" [5:08].
- He assumes a "50% chance of winning, 50% chance of losing" on every trade [5:08].
- Relative position sizes: entry 2 is about half of entry 1, and entry 3 is "way bigger". Exact risk percent is not given.
- Track-record claim: September 2023, about 14-15 lab webinars, 10 trades, 9 winners, "over six figures". With a win on October 1 that becomes 10 of 11 [3:06-4:07].

- Keep a core four pairs: "I only like have four on my watch list each week that I'm taking seriously" [2:06].
- Don't wait and miss the move: "I will literally enter for a sell right then right there because I don't want to miss the trade" [2:06].
- Use alerts: "set a TradingView alert for 1950, 1952 once it's closer to that stop-loss level" [6:08].
- Second entry is smaller: "that second position is a little bit lighter even though it's the better entry" [6:08].

- Accept the binary outcome: "it can only do one of two things: hit that take profit or hit that stop loss" [5:08].
- No trading on vacation: "I don't trade on vacation" [10:13].

## 13. Changes from his earlier approach (as he states them)
- 2020 vs now: "I don't care about sniper entries, right, I did in 2020, that's why I made sniper entry videos part one, part two, part three, part four" [8:11-9:11]. Now he goes for "the most pips" and doesn't miss trades. He only gets a "sniper" entry on the third, post-lower-high add.
- Early in his career he would write a setup in his plan, check back hours later and find he had missed the move. Entering at market immediately is "a way for me to combat that" [3:06].
- He says he has used this routine "for the last few years" and doesn't see a need to change it [10:13].
