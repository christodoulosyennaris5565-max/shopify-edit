## 1. What this video covers
This is his "position trading master class" for 2026 and the most mechanical description of his current method. He defines the four trading styles and their timeframes and argues for position trading using style success-rate stats. He then walks through his USDJPY chart: psychological round-number levels drawn as boxes, trend lines, 8 and 14 EMAs, and a Fibonacci A-B-C. He gives his entry method (laddered buy limits at 38.2 / 50 / 61.8, with the biggest lot at the deepest level), stop placement (midway between 78.6 and 100), TP ladder (0%, -27, -61.8, next big psych level) with alerts, moving the stop into profit after a higher low, and re-entries. He also covers swap costs, prop-firm account-size rules and a GBPAUD short. He also states the core directional rule: decide where price will be in 3 months and trade only in that direction.

## 2. Trading style, holding period and timeframes
His definitions of the four styles [1:03–2:04]:
- **Scalping:** 1-minute chart, in and out every 10-15 minutes.
- **Day trading:** about the 15-minute chart, out by end of day.
- **Swing trading:** about the 2-hour / 4-hour chart, out in a couple of days to maybe a week.
- **Position trading (his method):** daily / weekly chart. Hold "a week or 2 weeks or 3 weeks or 4 weeks or 6 weeks or 8 weeks … position trading goes longer and longer" [2:04].
- He reads the Fib and sets TPs on the **weekly** ("I go to the weekly time frame" [17:10]; "Let me go back to the weekly" for GA [21:13]).
- **Directional horizon:** 3 months. "Look at where you believe price will be 3 months from now, and only trade in that direction" [8:08].
- Example hold: a USDJPY move from late January to about mid-March ("a month and a month and a half") [19:12].

## 5. Analysis process, step by step
1. Strip the chart clean [5:06].
2. Draw the major psychological round-number levels as boxes (USDJPY: 150, 155, 160, plus higher targets) [5:06–6:06].
3. Add trend lines (inner/outer) to frame the trend [7:07].
4. Have the 8 and 14 EMAs on [7:07].
5. Draw the Fibonacci on the higher-timeframe A-B-C swing to see "exactly where price wants to go" [8:08].
6. Ask where price will be **3 months from now**, and only trade in that direction. For UJ he expected 163, then 167 by June/July, so only buys [8:08–10:08].
7. Plan the entry zone (38.2-61.8 limits), find confluence with trend-line bounces and psych levels, set the stop between 78.6 and 100, and set the TP ladder/alerts at 0, -27, -61.8 and the next big psych level [15:09–18:12, 23:14].
8. Check the reward is at least 3-5× the risk [6:06].
9. Take only A+ setups. If none exist, don't trade, even for a month [25:18].

## 6. Entry rules
- **Direction:** only with the 3-month bias. "Every trade I take should only be buys in that direction. There's no need for countertrend trading, no need for selling" [9:08].
- **Order type:** mostly **limit orders** (buy limits in an uptrend). "Your entries at least me as a position trader is more predicated on limits" [14:09].
- **Laddered limits across the Fib retracement:** once price has made its high (Fib 0%) and he expects a pullback, "I will get, you know, buy limits around this entire area… one that's around here, one that's around here, one that's around here" at about the **38.2, 50 and 61.8** [15:09].
- **Lot sizing across the ladder:** "the lowest one always has the highest lot size because … you're closer to where your stop loss is" [15:09]. The 38.2 entry gets a "more conservative" lot size [15:09]. The shallow limits make sure you're "at least in that trade" if price turns from 38.2 or 50. "If you're someone who only gets into the 61.8, you may miss it" [16:10].
- Sometimes he only uses 61.8 and 50 and misses a trade that turns at 38.2 [22:14–23:14]. Sometimes he places the limit on a **third trend-line bounce**, which may coincide with a psych level [23:14].
- **Confluence:** "maximize my confluences with Fibonacci, trend line … extensions or trend line bounces … and factor it all together to get where my entry should be" [23:14].
- **Alternative to limits:** set **alerts** at those levels and market-execute when notified. Fine for USDJPY/GU, where price is often still nearby hours later, but bad for gold, which moves too far [19:12–20:12]. "You probably got to have a balance of limits and alerts. But … I'll just do limits for a lot of the times and I may miss some trades" [22:14].
- **Re-entries:** if you don't want to hold one position for a month, take profit along the way and re-enter "when price gives you a higher low… retest the moving average, reenter" [11:08].
- "You are no longer looking for sniper entries" [6:06]. "Your entries do not matter when you're position trading… Cuz your stop loss is wide enough" [14:09].
- Several trades in the same direction are fine: "why would you not find 1 2 3 4 5 6 7 trades in that direction and just hold them?" [9:08].

## 7. Stop-loss rules
- **Placement:** "I usually have my stop losses in the middle between the 78.6 and the 100" [16:10]. The reason: price sometimes reaches 78.6 and still goes up.
- The same stop applies to all laddered entries (38.2, 50, 61.8) [17:10]. He mentions you could optionally raise it ("if you want to get it, you know, lower to the 61.8 or even a 50" [17:10]; wording unclear).
- **Width:** "You're no longer trying to have a 15 or a 25 pip stop loss… Your stop loss can be as big as you want it to be as long as it gives your trade enough breathing room, it fits the criteria, and your reward is at least three, four, five x that" [6:06].
- Example widths: about 400 pips on the UJ weekly example [20:12]. Typical ranges "80 pip stop loss, 400 pip TP… 50 pip stop loss, 250 pip TP… 100 pip stop loss, but a 500 pip TP", depending on pair [21:13].
- "A lot of you guys are not bad traders. Your stops are just way too tight" [16:10].
- Size the position to the wide stop: "you can't just have a 10 lot if your stop loss is this wide" [20:12].
- **Embrace drawdown:** be fine with 20, 30, 40 pips of drawdown [23:14].

## 8. Take-profit and targets
- He doesn't use just the -27 extension. "I'm not putting my TP as a 27 extension. I'm putting my TP higher … the -61.8. So now it's a one and 3.4 risk-to-reward ratio" [17:10].
- **TP ladder (with alerts at each):** **TP1 = Fib 0%** (the prior high), **TP2 = -27 extension**, **TP3 = -61.8 extension**, **TP4 = next big psychological level** (UJ 170, new all-time-high territory). With TP4 included it becomes about 1:4 R:R [18:12].
- Targets are big psych levels: "160, 150, 140 … 170" [18:12].
- GA weekly short: target 1.78, "before it gets back to two flat" (2.00). About 1:5.6 R:R [21:13].
- Minimum reward of 3-5× the risk [6:06].

## 9. Trade management
- **Partials:** "I will take TPs along the way" at each ladder level, "take money off the table" [18:12].
- **Stop into profit:** once price moves up and forms a higher low ("gave us a higher low"), move the stop into profit below it. Example: entries around 153, stop moved up so the trade is "400 pips in profit" (153 → 157) and "no longer a overall stop loss" (he jokes "1 in 17,000 risk reward") [18:12–19:12]. The worst case is then a stop-out in profit.
- **Let it play out over time:** "What are we being so impatient about? Let the market go" [19:12].
- **Alerts** at 0%, -27, -61.8 and 170 [18:12].
- **Re-enter** on higher lows / EMA retests if you've taken profit [11:08].
- **Overnight swaps:** "like a dollar, two dollars … At the end of a month, you may have paid what, 60, 80, 100 dollars in overnight fees. Not a big deal" [11:08]. Holding overnight and for weeks is normal.
- **Invalidation:** if price double-bottoms without a new low, the trend is intact (the January UJ example) [10:08].

## 10. Risk management and position sizing
- **R:R:** at least 1:3 to 1:5; examples 1:3.4 and 1:4 (UJ) and 1:5.6 (GA) [6:06, 17:10, 18:12, 21:13].
- **Lot sizing:** pick a stop you are comfortable losing in money terms. The deepest Fib entry (61.8) gets the largest lot and the shallow 38.2 the smallest [15:09, 20:12].
- **Pip value:** 3 lots = $30/pip. 700 pips × $30 = **$21,000** [10:08].
- **Account size, brokerage:** position trading works on a small personal brokerage account: $2,000-$10,000 ("$5,000 … $3,000, $4,000, $8,000, $10,000") [14:09, 22:14].
- **Account size, prop firm:** "only do this on a six-figure prop firm account, like a 100K account, 250K account, 500K account" [12:08]. Reason: daily loss limits. "If your daily loss on a 10K account is $500, like 5%, you're not going to be doing this because it's easy to lose 500 in a day" [12:08]. On a 500K account the max loss of 10% is $50,000, which he says you have never lost in a month [13:08].
- Prop-firm prices quoted (his firm, tradeprime.com): 100K = $825 (about $412-475 with codes); 500K two-step = $3,685 (about 30-50% off with codes; "about $2,000" discounted) [12:08–13:08, 22:14].
- **Plan:** pass and withdraw from a big prop account, then put part of the first withdrawal into a personal brokerage account to position trade on your own [13:08–14:09].
- **Trade selection:** "If it's not an A+ setup, I'm not trading. Even if that means I can't trade for a month, so be it because month number two will pay for months 1 2 3 4 and 5" [25:18].
- **Success-rate stats he quotes (2-year period):** scalping 1-3%, day trading 5-10%, swing 15-20%, position 25-30% [3:05].
