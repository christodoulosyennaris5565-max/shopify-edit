## 1. What this video covers
He defines scalping, intraday and swing trading by holding time and explains why he is an "intraday swing trader" and not a scalper. He then walks through a 4H chart (apparently AUDUSD [?]) showing how he trades with the trend from level to level: buy the break, take a partial on the pullback, move the stop into profit, scale back in, and close everything by Friday because he doesn't hold over a week. He also shows how he projects targets by cloning the first leg of a trend and using Fibonacci (entry area at 38.2/61.8, target at the -27 extension), and how he uses a side-by-side black 4H chart and 1H/15m chart to time entries on a bullish confirmation candle plus a moving-average crossover. This is the 2020 intraday/swing version.

## 2. Trading style, holding period and timeframes
- Definitions [1:02–2:03]:
  - **Scalper**: in the market 5, 15, 30 minutes, up to an hour; wants 20–30 pips (sometimes 40); trades 10–15 times a day on the 15-minute chart; exits at any sign like a doji or morning star [3:03].
  - **Intraday trader**: holds longer than an hour — 1, 2, 3, 4, up to ~12–15 hours.
  - **Swing trader**: holds a full day, 2–3 days, up to a week (he later says 2–4 days, and that swing can technically be months) [7:06].
- Himself: "I'm an intraday and a swing trader where sometimes my trades will last four hours, five hours, two hours, 18 hours, 15 hours, but sometimes my trades last two to three days" [2:03].
- Occasionally exits after ~30 minutes when something moves "ridiculously fast like gold and GA [GBPAUD]," but "my objective is not to get in and get out" [2:03–3:03].
- **Max holding period one week**: "I don't hold trades longer than a week; if it ends this Friday I'm closing my trade" [6:05], then re-enters after the next week opens.
- Timeframes in the example: daily/4H for structure and levels; 1H (60-minute) and 15-minute for the entry [14:10–15:10].

## 4. Tools and indicators (with exact settings)
- **Moving average** (period not stated) — scalpers flip when price goes above it [4:03]; his entry includes a "moving average crossover" [15:10] (implies two MAs; periods [shown on chart, not stated]).
- **Fibonacci retracement**: A–B–C legs; retracement at **38.2** (first example) and **61.8** (second); target the **-27 extension** [5:04], [8:06–9:08], [13:10].
- **Leg cloning**: copy the length of the first leg of a trend and project it from the pullback low to estimate the second leg's end [8:06–9:08].
- **Levels**: whole/quarter levels and mid-levels (e.g. "60 flat, 65 flat ... 625 is the mid level").
- **Boxes**: consolidation boxes; "breakout region" box template [11:09].
- **Black charts**: a black 4H chart side by side with a coloured lower-timeframe chart to keep emotions in check [14:10–15:10].
- Candle signals: hammer, morning star, bullish engulfing (confirmation).

## 5. Analysis process, step by step
1. On the daily/4H, identify the trend and the key levels and mid-levels price is moving between [5:04], [10:08].
2. Identify the legs of the trend: first leg (impulse), retracement, second leg; possibly a third push, then the trend "is done" [8:06], [11:09].
3. Draw a Fibonacci from the leg (A→B) and mark the retracement (C) at 38.2 or 61.8; mark the **-27 extension** as the target [5:04], [9:08].
4. Clone the first leg's length from the retracement low; if it lines up with the -27 extension, that is extra confluence for the take-profit [9:08], [13:10].
5. When a leg completes, re-draw: "the new C and D that was the old one becomes the new A and B" [13:10].
6. Plan the invalidation: if price closes below the 61.8 level (and the box/support) and retests, stop buying and look to sell to the next mid-level [11:09–12:09].
7. Open two charts side by side — black 4H and a 1H — then drop to the 15m to time the entry [14:10–15:10].

## 6. Entry rules
- Trade with the trend: "I like to go with the trend ... if I see price breaking this level I'd like to sell all the way down" [4:03].
- Buy the break of a level in an uptrend ("price broke up, broke above, I'm buying, I'm just letting it run") [5:04].
- Re-entry/scale-in: on the pullback to the 61.8 / support level, wait for "some type of bullish confirmation" — a **hammer or morning star**, or close + **bullish engulfing** — plus a **moving-average crossover**, on the 1H then 15m [13:10–15:10].
- Don't enter mid-candle like scalpers; scalpers "don't even wait for price to close" [3:03] (contrast with his approach).
- Order type: not specified.

## 7. Stop-loss rules
- Stop below the last low (for longs) [6:05].
- After a pullback makes a higher low, move the stop below that new low [6:05].
- "When price makes that high, moving my stop loss into profit, I'm scaling back in" [4:03].

## 8. Take-profit and targets
- Trade "from this level to this level no matter what; I don't care how long it takes" [6:05].
- Next level or the mid-level between levels [7:06].
- Fibonacci **-27 extension**, ideally matching the cloned first-leg length [9:08], [13:10].
- Target is still valid if price pulls back to 61.8: "don't think it's a failed trade unless price breaks structure" [9:08].
- Riding "the first leg of a trend" is also a valid intraday goal [8:06].

## 9. Trade management
- **Partials and scaling in**: "when it comes back up I take partial profit, but more times than not, when price makes that high, moving my stop loss into profit, I'm scaling back in for another entry and I'm selling it all the way back down" [4:03]. Partial % not stated.
- **Weekly close**: closes all trades on Friday ("I don't hold trades longer than a week"); next week, once the open settles ("when it stops moving around a little bit"), re-enters in the same direction with the stop below the same low [6:05].
- Adapt: if price closes below 61.8 and the support box, then retests, switch to selling toward the next mid-level (about "62-500") [11:09–12:09].
- Rollover fees: holding for months costs ~$2–5/day — "let's say you make 20 grand, two grand may go to the broker" [7:06].

## 10. Risk management and position sizing
- Frequency: "I'd rather get 200, 300, 400 pips in a week and only trade two or three times than trade 10 to 15 times" [4:03–5:04].
- Contrasts with scalpers' "15 trades a week ... 30 to 60 trades a month" [6:05].
- Risk %, lot size and R:R: not covered.

## 11. Trades walked through in this video

### Trade 1: Scalper's view of a 15m chart (illustration) (3:03–4:03)
- 15-minute chart (instrument not stated). He narrates what a scalper would do: sell a double top on a small doji for 20–30 pips, exit on a doji/morning star, enter mid-breakout of a box without waiting for the close, buy back above the MA, sell the next double/triple top. He uses it to show why scalping is "prone to a lot of fake outs." Not a trade he takes.

### Trade 2: AUDUSD [?] 4H uptrend, level-to-level long with scale-ins (5:04–7:06)
- Daily → 4H. Price bounces off a level, then breaks above it → he buys ("the trade that I definitely made") and lets it run.
- Price pulls back; he moves the stop below the new low and scales in again.
- Fib A→B with C at **38.2**; price went "right to the 27 extension and stop."
- This is "a whole month of movement," traded as a series of trades: close every Friday, re-enter the following week with the stop below the same low, until price reaches the target level.
- Entry/stop prices not stated [shown on chart, not stated]. Result: target reached (-27 extension).

### Trade 3: AUDUSD [?] second leg to the -27 extension (8:06–15:10)
- 4H. First leg identified and cloned; second-leg Fib A–B–C with C at **61.8**; earlier in the chart an A–B–C, lower high, double bottom, then a run to the -27 extension.
- Plan: price may come back to 61.8 (and the low that matches an earlier low); wait on the 1H/15m for a hammer/morning star/bullish engulfing and an MA crossover, then buy, target the -27 extension, which also matches the cloned leg length.
- Invalidation: a close below 61.8 and the consolidation/breakout box, then a retest → stop buying, sell toward the mid-level (~0.6250 [?]).
- Live/forward-looking; no result given. Levels named: "60 flat, 65 flat, 625 mid level."

## 12. Rules, do's and don'ts
- Not a scalper: "my objective is not to get in and get out" [3:03].
- Go with the trend: "I like to go with the trend, I don't like to be faked out" [4:03].
- Fewer trades, more pips: "I'd rather get 200, 300, 400 pips in a week and only trade two or three times" [4:03].
- Partials + stop to profit + scale in [4:03].
- Weekly limit: "I don't hold trades longer than a week; if it ends this Friday I'm closing my trade" [6:05].
- Level to level: "I'm trading from this level to this level no matter what. I don't care how long it takes" [6:05].
- A pullback isn't failure: "don't think it's a failed trade unless price breaks structure" [9:08].
- Adapt: "if price breaks below 61.8 and closes ... and a retest and still goes down — hard time buying this pair; adjust to what the market is doing" [11:09].
- Use black charts to keep emotions in check [14:10–15:10].
- Wait for confirmation: "wait for that bullish confirmation and moving average crossover and then you are good to go" [15:10].
