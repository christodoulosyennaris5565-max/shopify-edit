## 1. What this video covers
A long moving-average guide. The first ~15 minutes are textbook material for beginners: what an MA is, SMA vs EMA, and the arithmetic for both. The rest covers how **he** uses MAs. He uses an **8 and 14 EMA crossover** (8 blue, 14 red) for entries and reversal spotting, judged on the **4H or daily**, and a **20 and 50 EMA** for long-term direction. MAs are always combined with psychological levels/support-resistance, trend lines and strong candles, and are only used in trending markets. Targets come from levels, never from MAs. This is his early-2021 approach. It already includes the 8/14 EMA pair that the project's era summary associates with 2023.

## 2. Trading style, holding period and timeframes
- He reads the **daily and 4H first** to get the direction "for the next week, the next two weeks", then trades in that direction. [19:16]
- **MA crossovers are judged on the 4H or daily**, "because it gives me an accurate confirmation on a higher time frame". [18:15–19:16]
- He warns against 15-minute crossovers, which give fake-outs. His example: entering on the 15m with a **10-pip stop** gets you stopped out by a retest. [19:16, 21:17]
- **Holding period:** "not that I'm going to hold it for three weeks. I personally like to hold some trades." For intraday traders, he says know the 1-3 week direction and "get out every two days, every three days, whenever price starts to retrace… take partial profit, take full profit, get back in". [20:17]
- He describes himself as an intraday trader "to a certain extent" still. As an intraday trader he holds for 1-4 hours ("I'm only trading for two hours… an hour… four hours, I'm not trading for four days or five days"). [27:18]
- If the weekly candle is expected to close bullish, he doesn't sell that week. [22:18]
- Daily charts in the video are used "only… because it's a lot easier… to give an example". [18:15, 29:19]

## 4. Tools and indicators (with exact settings)
- **His entry MAs:** **8 EMA (blue)** and **14 EMA (red)**, Exponential, on TradingView, saved as an indicator template ("8 and 14"). "I use a 8 and a 14 moving average… doesn't matter, I use it 8 and 14." [16:13] He says 9/15, 7/13, 10/20 and 20/50 are also fine for others.
  - Blue (8) crossing above red (14) means uptrend. Red crossing above blue means downtrend. [16:13]
- **His bias/direction MAs:** **20 EMA and 50 EMA**, "for a longer term direction of the market and to see where my key levels are going to be". He says 50/100 or 50/200 also work ("it's all preference"). [25:18, 30:20, 36:22]
- **Single-EMA option:** he also has a one-line template (captions: "aema"). Price above it means up, below means down. He says one line vs a crossover "is just a personal preference". [17:14]
- **Why EMA over SMA:** EMA reacts faster. On his example the 8/14 SMA crossed so late that price had already dropped "from 1.34 to 1.29… 500 pips you just missed". [24:18–25:18, 28:19–29:19]
- On longer settings he found the 20/50 **EMA** gave a cleaner trend read, while the 20/50 **SMA** gave false crossovers during consolidation. [25:18–27:18, 28:19]
- **Textbook-only (not his edge):** SMA = sum of closes ÷ periods. EMA = (today's close − yesterday's EMA) × multiplier + yesterday's EMA, with multiplier = 2 ÷ (periods + 1) (0.3333 for 5, 0.1818 for 10), seeded with an SMA. He works examples (16.6 → 16.73 → 18.46; GBPAUD 1.35440 SMA → 1.35293 EMA). [5:06–15:11]
- He uses the Fib tool only to find the **50% midpoint** of a range for a target, "not to use fibonacci at all". [35:21]

## 5. Analysis process, step by step
1. Read the **daily and 4H** for direction over the next 1-3 weeks. [19:16]
2. Check the **20/50 EMA** for the long-term trend (e.g. blue over red since May 2020 = up, despite retracements). [25:18, 31:20]
3. Mark **psychological levels/support-resistance** and **trend lines**. A third bounce validates a trend line; two touches are only a "potential trend line". [31:20, 34:21]
4. Use market structure (break-of-structure ideas). A pop below a trend line that does not break the last higher low is not a break. A real break needs price to break the trend line, retest it, and take out the low. [32:20–33:20]
5. Treat moves against the 20/50 EMA bias as **retracements, not reversals**, and use them as entry areas. [36:22]
6. At a level or trend line, wait for the confluence stack (§6) and use the **8/14 EMA crossover** as the trigger or scale-in signal.
7. Set targets at levels: the prior high, the range midpoint, the next psychological levels (§8).
8. Flip bias only after structure breaks: lower highs and lower lows after a retest, and price going below the trend-line-break point. [37:22]
- Don't trade consolidation. When MAs are tangled in price, "there's no trend right now and you shouldn't be in a trade". [24:18]

## 6. Entry rules
- **MAs are never the sole reason:** "you shouldn't get in the trade just because there's a moving average crossover." They are combined with a trend-line bounce, a support/resistance bounce, or a bullish engulfing. [17:14–18:15]
- **The entry recipe:** "We're looking at a bounce off of support or resistance, or even a breakthrough… we're looking for a strong candlestick, not small candlestick… and then we mix the moving average with it." [23:18]
- **His three main confluences:** "trend line, support and resistance, and candlestick formations". [33:20]
- **Aggressive entry (trend-line long):** (1) a bounce off a valid trend line (third touch), (2) a bullish candle such as an engulfing or morning star, (3) an 8/14 EMA bull crossover. Enter there. [33:20]
- **Conservative entry:** wait for price to break above the nearby resistance/psych area and **retest it as support**, then go long. [34:21]
- **Scaling in:** on each pullback in the direction of the 20/50 bias, wait for a fresh 8/14 crossover in the trend direction and add. "The moving average just helps to scale in." [35:21–36:22] In a downtrend, a break of a counter-trend line is "another entry right here, scale in and then sell". [30:20]
- He ignores 8/14 crossovers against the 20/50 bias: "the reason we're not looking for it down here… based on the 20 and the 50 EMA… we know that price coming down is mere retracements." [36:22]
- Order type: not stated (examples read as entries after confirmation).

## 7. Stop-loss rules
- For a long after a bounce off support: "let me place my stop loss below it" (below the support). [20:17]
- For a counter-trend-line break sell: "stop loss above the last high". [30:20]
- Avoid tight 15-minute stops. A 10-pip stop on a 15m entry gets taken out by retests. [19:16]
- No fixed pip number of his own is given.

## 8. Take-profit and targets
- "Moving averages don't tell you where your take profit should be… for take profit moving averages don't help with that. What helps with that: support and resistance, psychological levels." [30:20–31:20]
- **Prior high:** "price is so close to the high it made last month, that is a take profit area… we always want to make sure we take profit at the high, or partial profit at the high." [34:21–35:21]
- **Range midpoint:** use the Fib tool on the range to find the 50% point and take profit or partial profit there. Example: a range of 1.2145 to ~1.23-1.24 (captions: "1.23 about 430"), called "about a 200 pip range", gives a TP about **100 pips** from entry. [35:21]
- **Short targets after a bias flip:** psychological levels, plus "the trend line break's point B and point A", then lower psych levels such as **1.19 and 1.18**. [37:22–38:23]

## 9. Trade management
- Scale in on pullbacks with 8/14 crossovers in the trend direction (§6). Scale out and take partial and full profits. "I like to hold some trades and scale in, scale out, partial profit, full profit." [22:18]
- For intraday holders: exit when price starts to retrace, then re-enter in the bigger direction. [20:17]
- Don't close a good trade in panic on a lower-timeframe pullback to near break-even. That is a retracement, not a reversal. [21:17]
- Bias flip rule: if price breaks structure, takes out the low, makes lower highs and lower lows after a retest, and goes below the trend-line-break point, he switches to sells and waits for the 8/14 red-over-blue crossover to enter. [37:22]
- Break-even, alerts and weekend holding: not covered in this video.

## 12. Rules, do's and don'ts
- Use EMA, not SMA: "I personally like exponential moving averages only because they put a clear emphasis on what price has done recently." [16:13]
- MAs are confluence only: "Moving averages are not to be the main confluence." [17:14]
- Judge crossovers on the higher timeframe: "I personally like to look at a moving average crossover on a four hour or a daily." [18:15]
- Don't trade chop: "During consolidation you will see moving averages trade in between price, that means there's no trend right now and you shouldn't be in a trade." [24:18]
- Use the 8/14 and 20/50 for different jobs: "I use an 8 and a 14 EMA for entries… but I use a 20 and a 50 EMA for a longer term direction." [30:20]
- Targets come from levels: "Moving averages don't tell you where your take profit should be." [30:20]
- Validate trend lines: "a third bounce actually validates the trend line." [31:20]
- Take profit at the prior high: "we always want to make sure we take profit at the high, or partial profit at the high." [34:21]
- Don't fight the weekly candle: "if you know that weekly is going to close as a bullish candle there's no point in selling during that week." [22:18]
- Don't call it a reversal on the 15m: "they think it's a reversal because they're too busy analyzing on a 15-minute time frame." [20:17–21:17]
