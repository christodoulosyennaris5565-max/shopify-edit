## 1. What this video covers
This is an 85-minute New Year compilation mixing motivation, business/podcast talk and strategy clips. The strategy content covers:
- stacking confluences on a gold short (trend line, wicks to the trend line and to the 1950 psych level, a 2H lower high, an MA crossover)
- scaling a small account by slowly raising lot size
- taking profit when price is within a few pips of TP
- the 2023 routine of entering at market immediately, adding a lighter second entry near the stop and a much bigger third entry once there is a lower high
- moving the stop only to market structure (below the last higher low), or to break-even when 40-50 pips up
- market execution plus a buy/sell limit as the second entry
- risk of 2-4% (5-8% aggressive, he sometimes goes to 9%), 1:3+ R:R and win-rate maths
- wider stops (40-70 pips) aiming at 140-230 pips
- journaling and changing bias when the market disagrees

Overall it reflects his 2023 swing approach. The structure-based stop tutorial may come from his earlier teaching.

## 2. Trading style, holding period and timeframes
- Swing trader who accepts drawdown: "me as a swing trader, being in drawdown does not scare me at all" [40:31].
- Typical stops: "my stop losses are usually 40, 50, 60 pips because I aim for 180, 200, 230 take profits" [26:24], and "chances are my stop loss is probably going to be like 50 pips, 60 pips, 70 pips" [40:31].
- Hold for 1-3 days: give the stop "an extra 20, 25, 30 pips ... and move the take profit to an area where you can hold it over a span of 1, 2, 3 days" [51:37].
- Timeframes mentioned: 2-hour (used to spot a lower high on gold) [9:09], and daily ("my charts right now ... on a daily timeframe") in the journaling clip [74:56].
- Scalpers with 7-10 pip stops can't use his drawdown/limit approach [40:31].

## 5. Analysis process, step by step
Pieced together from the clips:
1. Read the news/sentiment (TradingView news feed) so the technicals don't contradict the fundamentals [71:55-72:55].
2. Draw the trend line and count wicks into it, and into the nearest psychological level (e.g. 1950 on gold) [9:09].
3. Drop to the 2-hour chart to look for a lower high (short) [9:09].
4. Check for a moving-average crossover [9:09].
5. Count confluences. He is happy to execute with 2-3. Waiting for 4-5+ is a psychology problem [5:06, 33:28, 39:31-40:31].
6. Check against the plan: 1:3 R:R (1:2 / 1:4 depending on the plan) and 2-3 confluences [30:26-31:26].
7. Be ready to flip bias if the market disagrees ("UJ is going down ... a day later ... UJ is going up") [72:55].
8. Journal every trade and review on the chart why it won or lost [73:56-74:56].

## 6. Entry rules
- **Market-execute early with 2-3 confluences.** "If I got two or three and I feel like ... the market is not ready to move yet but I know what it's going to do, sometimes I for sure will just market execute for my first trade" [40:31].
- **Second trade as a limit order.** "Then my second trade, what I will do is put a sell limit if I think it's going to go up a little bit, retrace and then come back down, or place a buy limit if I think it's going to come back down and then go up" [40:31]. So he is often in two trades that are close together, or three with one further away [41:32].
- **Routine version (repeat of 0yoVaP_NTFA)** [17:15-21:19]:
  1. Market entry now so he doesn't miss the move.
  2. A TradingView alert near the stop. If price reverses at the psych level, add a lighter second entry, "whether it's half the lot size".
  3. After a new lower high, move the stop there and add "a way bigger lot size".
- **Don't enter late.** Losing traders get in "eight confluences late ... as opposed to getting in on the second or third confluence" [5:06]. Don't chase. Example: gold 1897 → 1917, "the old me would have tried to get in at 1913" [30:26].
- **Entering too early can also fail.** See Trade 4 (USDJPY).

## 7. Stop-loss rules
- Typical stop size is 40-70 pips (see section 2). Give trades "room to breathe". A 5-10 pip stop is "one of the biggest issues that I see traders face daily". Add "an extra 20, 25, 30 pips" [50:36-51:37].
- **Trail only to structure** [21:19-25:24]. Once price retraces and turns back in your direction, move the stop "a few pips below" the last higher low (longs) [23:23]. For shorts, above the last lower high [65:47]. "You can't just move it anywhere because price can easily come up and then stop you back out; it's not in alignment with structure" [23:23-24:23].
- **Keep the stop where it started until structure forms** [63:46-64:46]. "Keep your stop loss in the negatives, like wherever it was where you started, until price creates structure."
- **Break-even exception.** If you are 40-50 pips in profit and price starts retracing, "you can at least move your stop loss into break-even, which is your entry" [64:46]. Then move from break-even to below the new higher low once price plants and turns up again.
- Why: if a structure-based stop is hit, "it is no longer an uptrend" [64:46-65:47]. Every trade can't be risk-free right away, "you'll get stopped out all the time" [65:47].
- In the routine clip: stop at 1961 on a 1943 gold short. Moved to the new lower high to make positions risk-free [19:16-21:19].

## 8. Take-profit and targets
- Target sizes: 180/200/230 pips with 40-60 pip stops [26:24]. In one example he was 140+ pips in profit after 30 pips of drawdown on gold ("like a 1 to 5") [50:36].
- **Take profit when price is close to TP.** "If the market is two, three, four pips or 40 pips [on gold] ... take profit if it's close to it" [16:15]. Example: TP 1950, price at 1947, entry 1933. Don't walk away and let it reverse into a loss. "The market gave you 140 plus pips, take the profit" [17:15].
- Partial profit at a psychological level or "mid level" is mentioned but not explained ("this is not the video for partial profit") [24:23].
- Gold short example target 1910/1900 [20:18].

## 9. Trade management
- Set and forget after entering. Alerts near the stop (1950/1952 on the gold example) [20:18].
- Scaling in: lighter second entry in drawdown (≈half size), much bigger third entry after the stop moves to a new lower high [20:18-21:19]. Or market entry plus a limit-order second entry [40:31].
- Trail the stop below each new higher low (above each lower high for shorts), drawing a Fibonacci retracement at each pullback [22:21-24:23, 64:46-65:47].
- Break-even when 40-50 pips up and price retraces [64:46].
- Close near TP rather than waiting the last few pips [16:15-17:15].
- Partials at psych/mid levels (no % given) [24:23].
- Guest clip [11:11-12:11] (not Swaggy): close a floating profit once it passes a written goal ("150 plus close").

## 10. Risk management and position sizing
- **Risk % per trade** [46:34-47:34]: "if you want a risk number, 2 to 4% is fine, 5 to 8% is aggressive." He doesn't stick to 1-2%: "sometimes I'll risk three or four. If the trade is amazing I'll even risk 5 to 8%, even 9%". He says this is "not the norm" and not what you should do. Example: on a $100,000 account he may lose $6,000-$8,000 on a trade, but winners are $30,000-$40,000, "so I can lose two or three and then have two winners and I'm golden".
- **R:R**: "always trade with the 1 to 3 plus risk reward ratio, 1 to 2 if you have to" [45:34-46:34].
- **Win rate**: "you can have a 30% win rate and still be profitable ... is it a 1 to 4, 1 to 5, 1 to 6" [32:27].
- **Small-account scaling** [9:09-11:11] (repeat of 8ub5s9): $300 account at 0.04 lots → +$150 to $450 → 0.05-0.07 lots toward $700. No standard lot on a small account ("all it takes is 40 pips and your account is blown"). The captions here say "0.004/0.005", a mishearing of 0.04/0.05.
- **Don't up size after a win streak** [25:24-27:25]. Lab members went 6/6 at small size ($10-40 wins at 0.10 lots), then jumped to 0.60 lots on trade 7 (USDJPY). With a 60-pip stop that cost $360 and blew small accounts. "Who told you to add a higher lot size and not 0.01 or 0.04?"
- Don't up size to copy big days: "you shouldn't look at it as I'mma up my lot size just to get to that 25k in a day" [32:27].
- Never blow an account after year 3-4 of trading. That is "really reckless", risking the whole account on one or two trades [33:28].
- Funded accounts: a $50k-100k account lets you use bigger size ("standard lot, 50 [0.50?]") and be happy with $900 or $1,800 wins [49:36-50:36]. Prop firms are "by far the easiest way" to become profitable [61:45-62:46].
- Track-record claims: 6/6 in the lab and up about $110k floating [1:03]. Sept 2023: 9/10 [18:16]. 7 of the last 8 trades [25:24].

### Trade 2: XAUUSD long, take profit near TP (around [16:15]-[17:15])
- Entry 1933, TP 1950. Price reached 1947 (about 140 pips of profit). Point: close it instead of waiting the last 3 points. Otherwise it "tanks all the way back down" below the entry. Illustrative. No actual result given.

### Trade 3: XAUUSD short, routine example (around [17:15]-[21:19]; same as 0yoVaP_NTFA)
- Market sell at 1943, stop 1961, target 1910/1900.

### Trade 4: USDJPY long, loss (around [25:24]-[27:25])
- Context: "trade number seven" in the lab after a 6/6 run (7 of the last 8 won).
- Setup: price in consolidation "for a very very long time", cleared the high, came back and retested, a new trend line, a key level he did not want broken, a wick. "All the signs of bullish momentum". He still thinks it is bullish long term.
- Entry: "I got in at the wrong time". He entered early, expecting a drawdown to the trend line before a move up [level shown on chart, not stated].
- Result: price "just cleared through" and stopped him out. Stop size not stated; the lot-size example uses a 60-pip stop.

### Trade 5: XAUUSD (direction not stated), 30 pips drawdown → 140+ pips (around [50:36])
- He was 30 pips in drawdown, then 140+ pips in profit. That is a realized ~1:5. "Not even including GU" (he also had a GBPUSD position). No levels given.

### Trade 6: XAUUSD long, not taken (around [29:26]-[30:26]; repeat of 8ub5s9)

### Example: illustrative long, stop trailing ($ example) (around [21:19]-[24:23])
- Entry at break-even, risk $500, target $2,500 (1:5). Price goes up to about +$900-1,000, retraces to about +$500, then turns up. Draw a Fibonacci retracement and move the stop a few pips below that new higher low. Worst case is now about +$600. Repeat at each higher low. A possible partial at a psych/mid level.

### Example: illustrative uptrend, stop to structure / break-even (around [63:46]-[66:47])
- Keep the original stop until a higher low forms. If 40-50 pips up and retracing, move to break-even. After price plants and turns up, trail below the new higher low. Mirror image for shorts. A break of the last higher low means the trend is over.

## 12. Rules, do's and don'ts
- Execute on 2-3 confluences: "worry about why you get in ... eight confluences late ... as opposed to getting in on the second or third confluence" [5:06].
- Market-execute plus a limit: "I will just market execute for my first trade and then my second trade ... put a sell limit ... or place a buy limit" [40:31].
- Don't miss the move: "I don't want to miss the trade, so me as a swing trader being in drawdown does not scare me at all" [40:31].
- Take profit when near TP: "take profit if it's close to it" [16:15].
- Second entry lighter: "that second position is a little bit lighter even though it's the better entry" [20:18].
- Trail to structure only: "your stop has to be below the last higher low" [24:23].
- Don't trail too early: "keep your stop loss in the negatives ... until price creates structure" [63:46].
- Break-even when well up: "if you are 40, 50 pips in profit you don't want to ... lose money" [64:46].
- Wider stops: "give your trade some room to breathe" [51:37].
- R:R: "always trade with the 1 to 3 plus risk reward ratio, 1 to 2 if you have to" [45:34].
- Risk: "2 to 4% is fine, 5 to 8% is aggressive" [46:34].
- Don't raise size after a winning streak: "who told you to add a higher lot size?" [26:24].
- Scale small accounts slowly: "slowly raise the lot size every now and then" [10:11].
- Change bias when the market disagrees: "it's okay to change your bias" [72:55].
- Journal: "I wasn't profitable until my fourth year and a half because I wasn't tracking and journaling" [73:56].
- Don't try to flip tiny accounts: "stop trying to turn a $20 account into $2,000" [38:30].
- Check the news: "to make sure I am not trading based off the technicals alone" [71:55].

## 13. Changes from his earlier approach (as he states them)
- He found a 2016-2017 video of himself trading and says "6 years later, 7 years later I still trade the same way" [42:32-43:33]. (This clip is probably from about 2022-2023.)
- He became profitable only after he started journaling and tracking (about year 4.5). Before that he used notes, Snapchat screenshots and a red notebook [73:56-74:56].
- Earlier in his career he would plan a trade, walk away and miss the move. Entering at market immediately fixes that [17:15-18:16].
- He used to blow accounts trying to flip $20-$100 accounts (2014-2015) [38:30-39:31].
- He once was a day trader and then stopped, before the $5,000 TV-money account that he scaled up (goal: $1,300/month for bills) [3:04-4:05].
