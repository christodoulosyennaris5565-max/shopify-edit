## 1. What this video covers
He lists the mandatory items of a written trading plan and fills in each with his own answers: his "why," three confluences before entry, which sessions and 2–3 pairs to trade, timeframes (top-down 4H → 1H/2H → 15m/30m), exit rules, risk management (max lot size, minimum 1:3 R:R), how to journal trades, and whether to trade news. He ends with a sample sign-off statement of his own plan. This is the 2020 "intraday swing" version of his strategy. He says the plan he has now "is the same trading plan I had when I was broke." The personal content (debt, Mexico trip, people asking for money) at 11:10–13:10 is not strategy.

## 2. Trading style, holding period and timeframes
- He calls himself an "intraday swing trader" [5:05].
- Top-down: "I analyze on the four hour time frame ... I need that confirmation on like a one hour, two hour time frame, then I enter on like the 15 or 30 minute time frame" [5:05].
- In his sign-off: "I only analyze on the four hour and in a one-hour timeframe for confirmation" [8:06].
- The timeframe must match the style; e.g. a scalper analyzes on the 1H and enters on the 5m or 1m [5:05].
- Holding period: not stated in this video.

## 3. Markets, pairs and sessions
- Pick "two or three pairs you want to trade and try to master," find which session they move in, keep them if it fits your schedule, drop them if not [4:04].
- Plan around your schedule: trade pairs that move when you're available [4:04].
- His own sample plan: "I will only trade Asian session, the end of it, because of gold, and the beginning of London session for Great Britain [GBP pairs]" [9:07]. He also says "I won't trade after 3 p.m." (then corrects himself to the session statement) [9:07]. Time zone not stated (he lived in Los Angeles at the time, so likely Pacific [?]).
- Examples of pairs named: EURUSD, gold.
- Notes that London session is the middle of the night in the US [3:04–4:04].

## 4. Tools and indicators (with exact settings)
- His sample plan: "I won't use any indicators" [9:07] (pure price action).
- Fibonacci levels for exits: the "27 extension" (−27%) or 61.8 [6:06].
- Trade journal: automated software such as Myfxbook, or a written record of entry, exit, profit/loss in pips [7:06].
- Candlestick patterns as confluences: evening star, doji, bullish engulfing, shooting star.

## 5. Analysis process, step by step
His plan-building order (the items he says every plan must have):
1. State your name and your "why" (bigger than "to make money"); read it daily [2:04].
2. Decide how many confluences you need before entering (his: three) [3:04].
3. Decide when you trade: every day or not, how often, which session(s) [3:04].
4. Choose 2–3 pairs to master, matching their active session to your schedule [4:04].
5. Choose timeframes consistent with your style (his: analyze 4H, confirm 1H/2H, enter 15m/30m) [4:04–5:05].
6. Define exit rules: stop/what makes you exit, and take-profit method (next S/R, Fib −27 or 61.8) [5:05–6:06].
7. Define risk management: starting capital, maximum lot size, minimum R:R [7:06].
8. Decide how you'll document trades (Myfxbook or manual) [7:06].
9. Decide whether you trade the news (fundamental vs technical) [8:06].
10. Write it all down, sign it, read it every day [8:06–9:07].

For chart analysis itself: top-down from the 4H to the 1H/2H for confirmation and the 15m/30m for entry [5:05].

## 6. Entry rules
- **Three confluences minimum**: "I need to see, you know, a trendline break and like, you know, evening star, and then it broke through support at the same time ... I'm not getting in if I see one ... or two confluences; I need three no matter what" [3:04].
- Two-confluence examples he would reject: doji + bullish engulfing; bounce off resistance + shooting star [3:04].
- Reason: "I've seen more profit and better consistency when I had three confluences" [3:04].
- Enter on the 15m/30m after 4H analysis and 1H/2H confirmation [5:05].
- Order type: not covered.

## 7. Stop-loss rules
- Accept the stop decided before entry: "I'm never gonna risk more than I'm willing to lose, so if it goes against me it's okay ... there's no need for me to close; just let it hit my stop loss or watch it play out" [6:06].
- Stop placement method not specified here; he asks the viewer to decide what makes them exit (e.g. price retracing 20–30 pips against you) [5:05].

## 8. Take-profit and targets
- Options he lists: "the next support and the next resistance," "the 27 extension on Fibonacci or 61.8" [5:05–6:06].
- Minimum R:R in his plan: "I won't take a trade less than one to three" [8:06–9:07].
- Let trades reach the TP instead of closing early because "I made enough" [6:06].

## 9. Trade management
- Stand firm in drawdown if risk was sized correctly; don't close early out of fear [6:06].
- After a set number of losses: no revenge trading [9:07].
- Weekly and daily goals: aim for them, "if I don't hit it it's okay" [9:07].
- No break-even/partials/trailing mentioned.

## 10. Risk management and position sizing
- "Probably the most important thing on this list" [7:06]: how much you start with, maximum lot size per trade, R:R on every trade.
- Lot size must fit account size; example of what not to do: a 20 or 30 standard-lot position where 20 pips against you = −$3,000 or −$30,000 [6:06–7:06]. (The math in the audio is loose: 20 lots × 20 pips ≈ $4,000.)
- Example rules to write: "I will only use a 0.10 lot size," "I will only use a standard lot size," depending on capital [7:06].
- Minimum R:R 1:3 [8:06–9:07].
- Track monthly % growth/loss (e.g. +10%, +15%, −5%) in a journal [7:06].
- No specific risk % stated.

## 12. Rules, do's and don'ts
- Three confluences: "I need three no matter what" [3:04].
- Master few pairs: "figure out what two or three pairs you want to trade and try to master" [4:04].
- Timeframes must match style: "you can't just be like, oh I'm a scalper but you're analyzing on a daily time frame" [4:04].
- Have exit rules: "everybody focuses on entry ... nobody figures out when you're in a trade, you got to have a strategy and rules on getting out" [6:06].
- Respect your stop: "just let it hit my stop loss or watch it play out" [6:06].
- R:R floor: "I won't take a trade less than one to three" [8:06].
- Size to your account: "my risk management is in line with how much money I have in my account" [7:06].
- Journal every trade (Myfxbook or written) [7:06].
- Don't trade news (NFP 8:30 on the first Friday, FOMC) unless you've studied fundamentals [8:06].
- Sample plan: no indicators; only the end of the Asian session (gold) and the start of London (GBP) [9:07].
- No revenge trading, no greed [9:07].
- Stop switching strategies: "if you're one, two years in you already need to have an actual strategy ... and just need to execute it ... if it's failing, adjust a little bit" [10:08].
- Read your plan every day [9:07].

## 13. Changes from his earlier approach (as he states them)
