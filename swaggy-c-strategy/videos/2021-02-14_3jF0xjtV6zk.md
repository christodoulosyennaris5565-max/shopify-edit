## 1. What this video covers
A "remastered" version of his Fibonacci retracement video from about 9-10 months earlier. He skips the maths and gives his personal list of Fib rules: which timeframes he uses it on, which levels he draws, how he picks the swing, what has to happen at a level before price reverses, his two stop-loss options, and his take-profit (the -27 extension, sometimes the -61.8 extension). He works through EURUSD examples with the bar-replay tool, mostly on the daily chart so the examples run faster, while saying he really trades it on the 4H/2H. This is his early-2021 intraday/swing approach: Fib is one confluence that has to line up with a key level, the moving averages or a candlestick pattern.

## 2. Trading style, holding period and timeframes
- **His Fib timeframes:** only the **4H, 2H and 1H**. "I don't use a fibonacci retracement on anything lower than an hourly time frame. I only use it on the four hour, the two hour and the one hour." [3:05]
- **Daily Fibs:** he uses them rarely and accepts that they take **about 2 to 3.5 weeks, up to a month,** to reach the target. Example: the swing started 14 September, retraced from 28 September, hit the 50% on 1 October and the -27 extension on 22 October ("about a full month") [4:05]. The year is not stated, probably 2020.
- When he trades a daily Fib he holds it "till Friday", closes, and re-enters on Sunday or Monday until the move completes. He calls these "rare instances". [4:05]
- He says intraday traders should stick to 1H-4H, and that "the daily works as well" for swing traders [4:05]. (The same passage also says swing traders get less use from Fib because they are in and out fast, so it contradicts itself; see §14.)
- He calls himself someone who monitors an open trade every 1-2 hours. If he holds a trade overnight, the first thing he does on waking is check whether it was stopped out or hit target. [14:11]

## 3. Markets, pairs and sessions
- **EURUSD:** "a pair that likes to use the fibonacci a lot". All the main examples are on it. [22:16]
- **AUDUSD:** he gives it as a pair where Fib is not useful, because "it just flies straight up" and there is nowhere to draw one. [22:16]
- He mentions a first quick example on what the captions write as "tpp aud" [6:06], most likely **GBPAUD** [?].
- General rule: some pairs respect Fib more than others, so test it pair by pair.
- No sessions or times of day are covered.

## 4. Tools and indicators (with exact settings)
- **TradingView Fib retracement tool** with these levels: **0%, 38.2, 50, 61.8, 78.6, 100**. He has removed **23.6**: "23.6 is irrelevant." [4:05–5:05]
- **Extensions:** he targets the **"27 extension"** (the -0.27 / 127.2% extension) and the **"61.8 extension"** (the -0.618 / 161.8% extension). The exact extension values in his template are not read out. They are "[shown on chart, not stated]" beyond the names he uses.
- **The three "safe" reversal zones** are 38.2, 50 and 61.8. He says 50 is not a true Fibonacci ratio but sits midway between 38.2 and 61.8. 78.6 is "never irrelevant" but he uses it mainly for the conservative stop. [5:05–6:06]
- **Moving averages:** he refers to an MA crossover and MA retest as confluence, and the chart shows MAs. No periods are stated in this video. (His MA video from the same month gives 8/14 EMA and 20/50 EMA.)
- **Bar replay** is used for teaching, and a **counter-trend line** is drawn on the retracement.
- He keeps his charts clean. He criticises charts with "10 support and resistances… 20 lines… it looks like a coloring book". [16:12]

## 5. Analysis process, step by step
1. Mark the major key levels and support/resistance (he keeps a few "major areas" on the chart and nothing else). [24:20]
2. Make sure the Fib **starts from a confluence**, not "the middle of nowhere": a key level, an MA crossover, an MA retest, a trend-line break, a double bottom/top, etc. "Fibonacci has to be at a low level or a high level… it has to be in confluence with something else." [6:06–7:07]
3. Draw the swing (A→B). In a downtrend draw from the top down to the last low before the retracement. In an uptrend draw from the bottom up to "the highest point" before a **full** retracement. One small opposite candle does not count. A real retracement is about **4-6 candles** against the trend; 1-2 candles that only reach 23.6 are not one. [2:04, 6:06, 7:07–8:07]
4. Watch for the retracement (point C) to reach **38.2, 50 or 61.8**.
5. Wait for reversal evidence at that level. Either **"four to five hits"** on the level, or a **double bottom/double top** ("it's one or the other"). [8:07–9:08, 13:11]
6. Look for more confluence at C: a break of a counter-trend line drawn on the retracement, an MA crossover or MA retest, a candlestick pattern (e.g. bullish engulfing), C retesting the **point B of an earlier Fib**, or a pattern such as an inverse head and shoulders. [12:10–13:11, 26:21–27:22]
7. Enter. Place the stop (conservative or aggressive, §7) and target the -27 extension (§8).
8. When the target is hit the Fib is "done". Remove it and draw the next one, e.g. from the next MA retest or swing low up to the new high. [8:07, 12:10]

## 6. Entry rules
- Enter at a 38.2, 50 or 61.8 retracement **only** with confluence. Fib on its own is not enough: "you can't use fibonacci on its own, it's already affected in support and resistance." [25:20]
- Reversal confirmation means the level gets about 4-5 touches, or a double bottom/top forms there. [8:07]
- Example trigger: "you get your entry, you do a long position, you get in a mix of this moving average crossover and its bullish engulfing." [11:08–12:10]
- Counter-trend line: draw a counter-trend line on the retracement. Price should respect it ("boom boom boom") and then "finally break above, retest above, then head all the way up". [13:11]
- Order type (market vs limit) is not stated. The examples read as market entries after confirmation.
- Scaling in is not covered in this video.

## 7. Stop-loss rules
He has two options and says the choice "really depends on the trade". [10:08–11:08]
- **Conservative:** beyond the **78.6** retracement, which gives "all the breathing room in the world". If price goes through 38.2, 50, 61.8 and 78.6 and keeps going, "I'm cool with getting stopped out". [10:08–11:08]
- **Aggressive:** beyond the **last lower high (downtrend) or higher low (uptrend)**, "maybe even a few pips below to give it some breathing room". [11:08]
- If price reverses from 38.2 or 50 off a clear low/double bottom, the stop goes a few pips below that low rather than below 78.6. [11:08]
- He notes that other courses use other stops ("very tight above the last swing high/low", "above the 78.6", "always 20 pips"). [10:08]

## 8. Take-profit and targets
- **Main target: the -27 extension.** "When price hits the 27 extension I usually get out the trade." [13:11] "For me personally I am done with the trade, I am gone." [14:11]
- **Extended target: the -61.8 extension**, used only when momentum is strong at the -27. If he sees "huge candles being thrown at the market at the 27 extension" he holds for the 61.8 extension. If he sees small candles there he takes profit. [13:11–14:11]
- **Example R:R** for a long from an MA crossover and bullish engulfing with the stop a few pips below the low: the -27 extension gave about **2.1:1** and the -61.8 extension about **3:1** (captions say "30.21" [?]). [12:10]
- He explains why price reverses at the -27/-61.8: traders close positions there, so price "should instantly consolidate and retrace once they hit the 27 extension or the 61.8". [18:15–19:15]

## 9. Trade management
- **Early exit near 0%:** after point C, price either goes **straight to the -27 extension with no heavy retracement**, or it stalls and slides just past the **0% level** and retraces. If it is slow and choppy around 0% ("it's doing too much"), he gets out and does not wait for the -27. If it leaves C "with so much force and velocity and momentum", he leaves the trade on. [20:15–21:15]
- **Monitoring:** he checks an active trade every hour or every couple of hours, and first thing in the morning after holding overnight. [14:11]
- **Daily-Fib trades:** he closes on Friday and re-enters Sunday/Monday until the move completes (no weekend holding in that case). [4:05]
- Break-even, partials and trailing are not covered. "Play it by ear" is his stated general rule. [21:15]

## 10. Risk management and position sizing
Not covered in this video, beyond the example R:R figures in §8 (about 2.1:1 to the -27 extension and about 3:1 to the -61.8 extension).

## 12. Rules, do's and don'ts
- Only use Fib on the 1H, 2H and 4H: "I don't use a fibonacci retracement on anything lower than an hourly time frame." [3:05]
- Expect a daily Fib to take weeks: "it's going to take about two or three and a half weeks to actually fulfill." [3:05]
- Ignore 23.6: "23.6 is irrelevant. It's not a real retracement." [5:05–6:06]
- Fib needs confluence: "It has to start at a key level, start at a moving average crossover or moving average retest, break of a trend line… you can't just be in the middle of nowhere." [6:06–7:07]
- Draw to the true swing extreme: "You need an actual full-on retracement." [8:07]
- Confirm the reversal: it "needs about four to five hits on that level… or it has to be a double bottom or a double top." [8:07]
- Conservative stop: "your stop-loss should either be above the 78.6 retracement for a conservative stop-loss." [10:08]
- Aggressive stop: "above the last lower high or higher low." [11:08]
- Default exit: "When price hits the 27 extension I usually get out the trade." [13:11]
- Hold for -61.8 only on momentum: "if I see huge candles being thrown at the market at the 27 extension… I'm going to hold off of it to the 61.8 extension." [14:11]
- Exit if price stalls around 0%: "it's doing too much, I'm getting out the trade." [20:15]
- Not every pair suits Fib: "you can't use fibonacci for everything." [22:16]
- Keep charts clean: "I have to keep my charts really clean." [16:12]
- Play it by ear: "the most important advice I can give you guys is to play it by ear." [21:15]
