## 1. What this video covers
This is his stated "2023 strategy" video, the yearly strategy explainer he had skipped earlier in 2023. He lays out a numbered list of points: take 60–75% off at TP1; enter only between 6:30 and 9:30 pm, in the low-volume Asian session; get in early and accept some drawdown rather than miss the move; close manually when price is a few pips from TP; use psychological levels; read candlesticks; use the 8 & 14 EMAs as his only indicators; and read fundamentals and sentiment, including DXY for gold. He illustrates the points with a live gold long that had just won and a GBPUSD trade, then places a real GBPUSD sell limit on camera with all the numbers. He says his strategy is "on a foundational level… the same" as 2020–2022 but different "from a technical level".

## 2. Trading style, holding period and timeframes
- **Style:** swing trading with some day-trading elements. Aimed at "swing traders and even day traders", not scalpers [7:05]. Trades are placed in the evening and often left running overnight and through the next day; the gold trade was entered Monday and moved on Wednesday morning [5:05].
- **Timeframes:** daily for psychological levels and the TP2 view [12:08, 24:17]; 4H and 2H for DXY and trendlines [18:12, 22:17]; Fibonacci on the lower-timeframe structure [23:17]. Entry timeframe: not stated.
- **Stops and targets:** stops are usually 40–60 pips, because "my take profit is so massive" [26:18–27:18]. Example R:R runs from 1:2.3 to 1:4.2.

## 3. Markets, pairs and sessions
- **Gold (XAUUSD)** is "my favorite pair always has been". He made about $25–26K on gold in the recent trades, plus $7.8K on GBPUSD [2:03].
- **GBPUSD** is used for the live example.
- **Entry window:** "I usually enter my trades between 6:30 p.m. and around 9:30 p.m.… like 99% of my trades" [4:04]. He calls this the Asian session [6:05]. Time zone not stated, presumably US Eastern [?].
- **Reason:** low volume and liquidity while the market consolidates. By London he has "already forgot about the trade", which helps his discipline [4:04–5:05].
- He calls himself "a London-ish New York overlap trader", meaning the moves happen then, "but I will never ever ever enter during those times" [4:04].
- **News:** FOMC was mentioned as a reason to exit GBPUSD at TP1 [3:04]. He watches UK CPI and the Fed rate decision [17:11].

## 4. Tools and indicators (with exact settings)
- **8 EMA and 14 EMA crossover.** "I got my eight and my 14 crossover two EMAs; these are the only indicators I use" [16:11]. He no longer uses RSI, MACD or stochastics; he used to, "eight years ago seven years ago when I first started trading".
- **Psychological levels (round numbers).** GBPUSD: 1.20, 1.25, 1.30, 1.35, 1.40 are the major whole levels, with mid levels such as 1.225. Minor levels like 1.23 or 1.24 count but are "not as major" [12:08–14:10]. Gold: 1850, 1900, 1950, 2000 [21:15]. A level is a zone: "price stopping at 1.25 and price stopping at 1.2547, that's the same thing" [12:08].
- **Extra rays and small boxes** where price clearly reacted, even if the level is not a psychological one (e.g. GBPUSD 1.185 and 1.237; gold 1925) [20:12–23:17]. He keeps these visually different from the major levels so he doesn't confuse them [21:15].
- **Trendlines and channels:** long-term trendlines, a descending parallel channel and consolidation boxes. He keeps an unbroken trendline on the chart even after price leaves it [21:15–22:17].
- **Fibonacci extension:** 27 (1.27) and 61.8 (1.618) used as TP1 and TP2. "Anywhere between this area 27 and 61" [23:17–24:17].
- **Heikin Ashi** candles to confirm the overall trend [28:18].
- **Candlestick patterns and closes:** what candle printed at the level and how it closed [15:10–16:11].
- **Fundamentals:** the TradingView news (lightning bolt icon) linking to DailyFX analyst articles, plus ForexFactory red-folder events [16:11–17:11].
- **DXY (dollar index)** on the 4H/2H as a confluence for gold [18:12].

## 5. Analysis process, step by step
1. Mark the psychological levels: major round numbers, mid levels, and minor round numbers to be aware of [12:08–15:10].
2. Add rays or small boxes where price has reacted in the past, plus trendlines, channels and consolidation boxes [20:12–23:17].
3. Read the candlesticks at those levels: the pattern, and how the candle closed [15:10–16:11].
4. Check the 8/14 EMA crossover [16:11].
5. Read the fundamental sentiment: the TradingView news, DailyFX analyst opinion and ForexFactory high-impact events. For gold, check the DXY: a weaker dollar means stronger gold [16:11–19:12].
6. Determine the structure (last lower high or higher low) for stop placement. Use Fib extensions to confirm that targets line up with psychological levels [20:12–24:17].
7. Check Heikin Ashi for trend direction [28:18].
8. Enter between 6:30 and 9:30 pm, early and before the move, accepting a little drawdown (see §6).

## 6. Entry rules
- **Timing:** enter only between 6:30 and 9:30 pm, during the low-volume Asian session [4:04].
- **Get in before the move.** Entering when the market is already moving is "a few confluences too late". "It is better to get in if you are confident the market is going to go in a direction and it's close to it but it may have a drawdown" [7:05]. He would rather market-sell now than wait for a perfect sell limit that may never fill [8:05].
- **Accept drawdown.** "I expect a little bit of drawdown" [8:05–9:05]. In the gold example he was about 20 pips in drawdown [5:05].
- **Don't miss trades.** "A lot of times you guys would be profitable if you just didn't miss the trade" [9:05].
- **Gold-specific confluence:** gold was already moving up and had reached the 1928 level, "all I had to wait for was a retest", backed by DXY weakness and the Fed pause expectation [19:12].
- **Retracement needed when the stop would be too far.** "I would need some type of retrace", otherwise the stop would sit too far up at the last lower high [20:12].
- **Order types:** market orders for the early entries. A sell limit at a reaction level plus Fib confluence for the GBPUSD example [23:17–26:18].
- **Number of entries:** the gold trade was two positions, a "13 lot and the six lot" [5:05] (13 and 6 lots as heard [?]).

## 7. Stop-loss rules
- The stop goes beyond structure: "my stop loss is always in accordance with structure. This is the last lower high right here, that's my stop loss, 10, 20 pips above that" [9:05].
- He loses because he was wrong, never because the stop was too tight: "you'll never see me lose because my stop loss was too tight" [9:05].
- Typical size: "I usually don't have 30 pip stop losses, I usually have 40 and 50 and 60 because my take profit is so massive" [26:18–27:18].

## 8. Take-profit and targets
- **TP1 and TP2.** At TP1, take 60–70% off, later stated as 60–75%, and let the other 25% run risk-free [3:04, 27:18].
- **Targets** are psychological levels that line up with Fib extensions: the 1.27 extension for TP1 and the 1.618 for TP2 [24:17].
- **Take profit when price is close.** If price is a few pips from TP (2–3 pips on forex, roughly 10–20 pips on gold), close it manually rather than wait [10:05–11:06].
- **Manual early TP at a minor round number,** e.g. take profit at 1.2300 rather than wait for 1.2250 [14:10–15:10].

## 9. Trade management
- **Partials:** take 60–75% off at TP1 and let the remaining 25% run. "It's risk-free at that point. If it stops you out at break even great" [27:18].
- **Break-even / profit lock:** "when I'm in a trade and I see that I'm in profit 100 pips, 90 pips, 120 pips, chances are structure was created and I'm able to move my stop loss into break even, into profit" [28:18–29:19]. He'd rather be stopped out in profit than give it all back.
- **Early exit for news or consolidation:** he closed GBPUSD at TP1 rather than run to TP2 because "it was consolidating… and FOMC wasn't too good" [3:04].
- **Structure-break exit:** if price prints a higher low and breaks up against a short, "get out get out because this is structure being broken" [25:18].
- **Holding overnight:** yes. Trades are entered in the evening and left to run while he sleeps [5:05].

## 12. Rules, do's and don'ts
- Take most of the position off at TP1. "You take maybe 60 to 70 percent of my trade off if it hits TP1" [3:04].
- Enter in the quiet evening window. "I usually enter my trades between 6:30 p.m. and around 9:30 p.m." [4:04].
- Never enter during London/NY. "I will never ever ever enter during those times" [4:04].
- Get in before the move. "You enter a few confluences too late when the market is moving" [7:05].
- Don't miss the trade. "I'm not missing a trade. Sometimes I'll lose of course" [9:05].
- Put the stop beyond structure. "My stop loss is always in accordance with structure" [9:05].
- Close when it's almost at TP. "Take profit if it's close to it" [10:05–11:06].
- Use only the EMAs. "These are the only indicators I use. I don't use RSI" [16:11].
- Read the fundamentals. "Look at all the fundamentals and see the sentiments" [18:12].
- Gold moves inversely to the dollar. "Anytime the dollar is weak gold is essentially very very strong" [18:12].
- Only mark levels that matter to you. "Make your markings where price stops at… make it where it makes sense to you" [22:17–23:17].
- Exit if structure breaks. "Get out get out because this is structure being broken" [25:18].
- Move the stop to break-even or profit once you are about 90–120 pips up and structure has formed [29:19].
